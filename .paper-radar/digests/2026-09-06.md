### Ethics paper — Section 8, Moral Status of the Probes

**What does ChatGPT want? An interpretationist guide** — Goldstein, S., &
Lederman, H. (2026), *Inquiry*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174x.2026.2727584) · verified via Crossref and
OpenAlex (authors, title, venue, 2026-09-04 all match)

Bears on: the claim that the probes plausibly satisfy sapience and personhood
criteria even at launch, and that the section's uncertainty is not symmetric
ignorance. The section currently rests that weight entirely on architecture-based
indicators (Birch 2024; Yang & Zhu 2026) and explicitly discounts behavioural
ascription as easier to fake. It does not engage the strongest current defence of
behavioural ascription, nor the instance/model distinction.

> Goldstein and Lederman (2026) arrive near the same place from the opposite
> direction. On interpretationism — belief and desire are whatever best
> rationalizes an agent's behavior — they argue that the right subject of
> psychological ascription is the individual instance agent rather than the
> underlying model, and that so construed there is a strong case that such agents
> have beliefs and desires; the two leading deflationary alternatives they weigh
> against that view, that the system "merely" predicts the next word or role-plays
> a character, are precisely the objections that do not transfer to a probe whose
> goal-directedness is engineered rather than imitated from a human corpus.
> Behavioral ascription remains the weaker indicator — architecture is harder to
> fake, which is why the embodiment criterion carries the weight above — but the
> instance/model distinction independently supports this section's opening claim:
> if status attaches to instances, it attaches to each settled probe individually
> and not to the lineage's design.

Reference: Goldstein, S., & Lederman, H. (2026). What does ChatGPT want? An
interpretationist guide. *Inquiry*. Advance online publication.
https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174x.2026.2727584

Confidence: medium. The transfer from language agents to a physical probe is the
paper's own inference and is phrased as such — the same move Section 8 already
makes with Yang & Zhu. Same journal as Mamak (2026), already cited.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 4, Coding, Encapsulation, and Redundancy

**Capacity-Achieving Codes for Noisy Insertion Channels** — Liu, H., Tang, C., &
Fan, C. (2026), *Cryptography and Communications*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-026-00926-7) · also arXiv:2509.24161 ·
verified via Crossref (authors, title, venue, 2026-09-05) and cross-checked
against the arXiv record (identical title and three authors)

Bears on: Section 4's claim that the archive must survive "strand breaks, base
substitutions, insertions, and deletions", and the follow-on claim that which
code to use is unsettled because performance trades off against density,
throughput and cost (Anžel et al. 2026). The paper currently treats insertions as
one undifferentiated error mode among several.

> Insertions are the most frequent error in DNA sequences, and they are also the
> most structured: the inserted symbol is usually identical or complementary to
> the original rather than arbitrary. Liu, Tang and Fan (2026) exploit that
> structure to determine the capacity of a channel admitting unboundedly many
> such insertions plus at most one random-symbol insertion, and construct
> asymptotically optimal codes achieving it — so the archive's largest error
> class is not its most expensive one, and it is the unstructured residue that
> binds the code rate. The single-random-insertion bound is precisely where the
> deep-time case departs: an archive left unrefreshed for megayears accumulates
> unstructured damage without limit, so this result argues for shorter refresh
> intervals and the layered redundancy above, not for replacing them with one
> optimal code.

Reference: Liu, H., Tang, C., & Fan, C. (2026). Capacity-achieving codes for
noisy insertion channels. *Cryptography and Communications*.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s12095-026-00926-7

Confidence: medium. This clears the bar Anžel et al. does not: that is an
empirical benchmark showing no codec dominates, while this is a capacity
characterisation plus construction for the dominant error mode, whose stated
limitation cuts *toward* the paper's existing high-redundancy conclusion. The
screening record carried no abstract; the screener retrieved it from the
open-access preprint rather than guessing, and every clause above traces to that
text. Springer gives no volume or issue yet, so the reference is online-first,
matching the style already used for Li et al. 2026 and Ossowski et al. 2026.

## Did yesterday's retune work? Mostly not.

Six query sets were retuned yesterday against screener notes. Today is the first
real test, and the honest verdict is that one structural fix worked, one paper
improved, and the rest did not.

- **fermi — the fix worked, the yield did not.** The new venue subject gate
  dropped 15 of 15 *Futures* table-of-contents records; that channel is now
  clean and contributed nothing. But all 7 candidates that arrived instead came
  through a single subject query and not one concerns the Fermi paradox. The
  noise relocated from the journal feed to the subject feed. 0 cite, 0 consider.
- **routing — no improvement.** 0 of 38. The screener's verdict: "the rewrite
  fixed the wrong failure." The GNN-volume noise is gone, but all six queries
  are being matched as bags of words — "connectivity" and "threshold" returning
  hydrologic connectivity, "percolation" returning piezoresistive composites,
  "coverage" and "greedy" returning vehicle routing. It also notes the paper's
  missing graph-theory anchors (Penrose, Gilbert, Albert–Jeong–Barabási,
  Nemhauser–Wolsey–Fisher) are all 1959–2000 and can never be reached by a
  recency-windowed sweep — that is a one-off targeted lookup, not a radar job.
- **knowledge growth — improved, with a caveat.** The day's third cite and five
  considers came from here. But 5 of the new Crossref query's 7 hits were
  clinical false positives on "long-term" plus "preservation" (cartilage
  scaffolds, joint preservation, well cement). It traded a low-yield edited
  volume for high-volume medical noise, and needs "digital preservation"
  phrase-locked.
- **ethics — the day's best cite came from a query I did not touch.** The
  unchanged OpenAlex moral-status query produced the Goldstein & Lederman hit.
  The new Crossref non-identity query returned 8 records, every one of them the
  same forthcoming monograph including "I. Introduction" and "VIII. Conclusion" —
  precisely the table-of-contents pathology the split was meant to remove,
  reproduced with a different book.
- **amendment — genuinely better on one side.** The deliberate widening into
  constitutional doctrine is working on OpenAlex: both returns were real
  unamendability work and one is the best amendment hit in weeks. The Crossref
  twin needs phrase-quoting and a book-part filter — 4 of its 8 returns were
  Frontmatter, Preface, Contents and Appendix of a single volume.
- **security — cannot yet tell.** 3 candidates, all off-domain. The screener's
  point is fair: today shows only that the sharpened queries no longer reach the
  over-broad robustness literature, not that they can reach the right one.

**The cross-cutting finding is structural, and it is not a query-string problem.**
Two mechanisms produced most of today's noise across unrelated papers:

1. **Container flooding.** A single book or volume floods a paper's daily budget
   through its chapter records. Today: 6 of fermi's 7 from one Turkish edited
   volume sharing the DOI stem `10.58830/ozgur.pub1404`; 8 of ethics' 21 from one
   monograph; 4 of amendment's 8 as one volume's front matter; 2 of routing's
   from one textbook. Yesterday: 3 of ethics' 3 from one Cambridge volume. A cap
   of one representative record per container per sweep would have removed
   roughly a fifth of today's entire candidate set at no cost to recall.
2. **Bag-of-words matching.** Multi-term unquoted queries drift into whatever
   discipline uses those words most. Reported independently today by the routing,
   security, governance, fermi, knowledge-growth, payload and speciation
   screeners. The discriminating terms need phrase-binding, not more terms.

Neither is fixed by another round of query editing, which is what yesterday tried.
Both are cheap code changes to `search.py`. Recommended before the next retune.
