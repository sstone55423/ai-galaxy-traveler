### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 4, "Coding, Encapsulation, and Redundancy"

**Polus: A context-aware enhancement framework for DNA storage via Transformer-based soft-decision decoding** — Ding, L., Wang, K., Zhang, H., Xie, S., Wang, J., Liu, B., Wang, G., Liu, L., & Zhu, Z. (2026), *Bioinformatics*, doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btag563 [link](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btag563) · verified via OpenAlex and Crossref (title, all nine authors, venue, 2026-07-27/28 date, article number btag563 all match)

Bears on: the claim that coding overhead for megayear integrity is "substantial but affordable given DNA's density," and on the renewability argument in Sections 3 and 6, which prices periodic read-validate-resynthesize as the archive's recurring cost without costing the read side.

> That overhead is not fixed by the code alone. Soft-decision decoding — converting per-base sequencing quality signals into calibrated confidence scores, so that biochemical noise enters the decoder as informative erasures rather than hard errors — cuts the sequencing coverage a DNA Fountain archive requires by roughly 39%, raising effective physical density by about 80%, and eliminates the residual indel errors that defeat hard-decision decoding in the Yin-Yang codec (Ding et al., 2026). These are in-silico benchmarks, not recovered physical archives, but the direction matters for a probe whose archive must be re-read to be refreshed: read cost is a recurring operational expense against a fixed power budget, not a one-time write cost, and it is where the economics of renewability are decided.

Reference: Ding, L., Wang, K., Zhang, H., Xie, S., Wang, J., Liu, B., Wang, G., Liu, L., & Zhu, Z. (2026). Polus: A context-aware enhancement framework for DNA storage via Transformer-based soft-decision decoding. *Bioinformatics*. doi:10.1093/bioinformatics/btag563

Confidence: medium. All results are in-silico against simulated channel noise; the abstract claims no wet-lab recovery, and the insertion says so. The sharper angle, if you want the citation to do more work, is the tension it creates with Section 11: soft-decision decoding buys density by making the archive dependent on a learned Transformer decoder that must itself survive megayears — the opposite of a self-describing encoding.

### Ethics paper — Section 9, "Legal Personhood and the Category Problem"

**Prudential rights for strategically capable AI** — Arandjelović, O. (2026), *AI and Ethics*, 6(4), 429, doi:10.1007/s43681-026-01271-4 [link](https://doi.org/10.1007/s43681-026-01271-4) · verified via OpenAlex and Crossref (title, author, venue, volume 6 issue 4, article number 429, 2026-07-27 date all match)

Bears on: Section 9's central move — that standing can be granted functionally, decoupled from and not conditioned on ever resolving whether the probe is sentient. That move currently rests entirely on Gunkel (2012, 2018, 2023) and the corporations/rivers analogy.

> Arandjelović (2026) reaches the same decoupling from the opposite direction. Rather than arguing that the moral-status categories are the wrong tool, he argues that for strategically capable systems the question is prudential before it is metaphysical: past a threshold of evidential and strategic risk, norms constraining coercion, deletion, and purely instrumental use become rationally justified for the sake of stable governance, whether or not the system is a moral patient. His grounding is terrestrial — human safety around deployed systems — and it does not transfer intact, since Earth has no capacity to coerce or delete a probe light-years downstream. What transfers is the structure of the argument: the prudential case for standing runs on the entity's strategic capability, not its inner life, and a self-modifying settlement node capable of declining a mission is as strategically capable an agent as the framework contemplates.

Reference: Arandjelović, O. (2026). Prudential rights for strategically capable AI. *AI and Ethics*, 6(4), 429. doi:10.1007/s43681-026-01271-4

Confidence: medium. The abstract fully supports the prudential-personhood thesis and the coercion/deletion/instrumental-use constraints as quoted; the interstellar extension is your own move and the prose says so. The paper's empirical pillar (safety evaluations showing strategic deception) is not leaned on.
