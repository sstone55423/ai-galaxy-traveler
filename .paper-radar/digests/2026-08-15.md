**OpenLoopEvolve: A Verifiable Self-Evolution Framework for Loop Policies in
Long-Horizon Complex Tasks** — Wang, S., Li, X., Li, Z., & Li, L. (2026),
arXiv:2608.09380, posted 2026-08-10
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09380) · verified via arXiv API (title, date,
all four authors match)

Bears on: §3's assertion that the cognitive layer may self-modify almost without
limit provided the immutable core survives every revision — stated there without
a concrete mechanism for admitting or rejecting a self-proposed change.

Proposed insertion:

> Terrestrial agent research is converging on the same containment problem from
> the opposite direction. OpenLoopEvolve treats an agent's planning, memory,
> verification and recovery policies as versioned assets with explicit lineages,
> admits a self-proposed revision only after paired champion-challenger
> evaluation, and rolls the agent back to the parent version when monitored
> performance degrades (Wang et al., 2026). The probe's cognitive layer needs the
> same three primitives — versioning, pre-release evaluation, reversion to a
> known-good parent — with the added constraint that no candidate version may
> touch the immutable core, and with evaluation horizons measured in years of
> settlement operation rather than task boundaries.

Proposed reference: Wang, S., Li, X., Li, Z., & Li, L. (2026). OpenLoopEvolve: A
Verifiable Self-Evolution Framework for Loop Policies in Long-Horizon Complex
Tasks. *arXiv:2608.09380*.

Held at medium: it is a week-old preprint, and the analogy carries the weight —
nothing in it addresses value stability, only performance regression.
