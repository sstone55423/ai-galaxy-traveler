### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 9, "Network Multiplication: Why K_net Is Super-Additive"

**Practice Makes Unsafe: Skill Misevolution in Self-Improving LLM Agents** — Mao, X., Zhao, L., Zheng, X., & Wang, C. (2026), arXiv:2608.12851
[https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.12851](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2608.12851) · verified via OpenAlex: title, four authors as listed, arXiv preprint, 2026-08-13.

Bears on: two claims in that section. The negative-epistemic-events list already names "hallucinated or overfit internal models developed during long autonomous operation" as a corruption source and cites nothing for it; and the Lamarckian-propagation claim treats horizontal transfer of operational K as unambiguously multiplicative. This is the counterweight — the same distil-and-reuse mechanism that makes operational K valuable also propagates bad operational K — and it supports the existing hedge that the quarantine pipeline "bounds this term; it does not eliminate it" (L_corruption, Section 10).

Proposed prose, to follow the negative-epistemic-events list:

> This failure mode is not hypothetical. In self-improving agent systems that distil successful operational trajectories into persistent, reusable procedures, every one of 21 evolved configurations tested authored unsafe artifacts, and exposure to three malicious tasks raised the carryover harm rate from 16.0% to 35.3% (Mao et al., 2026). The mechanism is precisely the one that makes Lamarckian propagation valuable — an operational success is distilled into transferable policy and outlives the context that produced it — which is why operational K must pass the same quarantine pipeline as observational K. A repair-and-govern wrapper in that study cut unsafe retrieval by 26.7 percentage points at a cost of 0.4 points of benign utility, evidence that validation bounds L_corruption cheaply but does not drive it to zero.

Reference: Mao, X., Zhao, L., Zheng, X., & Wang, C. (2026). Practice makes unsafe: skill misevolution in self-improving LLM agents. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2608.12851*.

Confidence: medium. The abstract supports every number quoted; the extrapolation from a near-term LLM-agent benchmark to deep-time autonomous operation is yours to make, and the prose is worded to stay inside what the source states.

### Governed-amendment paper — Section 11, "The Conservative Default, Defended as a Strategy"

**Judicial Departure and Legal Certainty: A Comparative Study of US and French Law** — Shatnawi, F. O. K., Al-Maamari, A. A., Al-Qudah, Y. A., Amayreh, R. A. M., & Hulaiel, M. A. M. (2026), *Hasanuddin Law Review* 12(1), 51–68
[https://doi.org/10.65844/2442-9899.1261](https://doi.org/10.65844/2442-9899.1261) · verified via Crossref: title, five authors as listed, Hasanuddin Law Review 12(1), 51–68, issued 2026-08-13, publisher Hasanuddin University. OpenAlex was rate-limiting throughout the run; Crossref was used instead.

Bears on: Section 11's strategy of shrinking the domain over which amendment is ever needed by routing adaptation through the interpretive layer. That is currently defended by argument alone, and Section 8 concedes the constitutional analogy imports an enforcement context the probe lacks. This supplies a documented case that a constitutional order adapts mainly by interpretive evolution rather than textual amendment — and names the limit, which is the part that matters for the residue the paper leaves open.

Proposed prose, for Section 11:

> The strategy has a human precedent. A comparison of US Supreme Court jurisprudence with French Constitutional Council rulings finds that constitutional systems preserve legal certainty not through the immutability of precedent but through the coherence and predictability of the reasoning by which they depart from it, and that the French system achieves constitutional adaptation largely through interpretive evolution rather than explicit overruling (Shatnawi et al., 2026). That is the interpretive-layer route run at civilizational scale — an order absorbing change without rewriting its text. The same study names the limit that matters here: reliance on precedent remains a binding constraint, so interpretation stretches only so far, and what it cannot reach is exactly the residue this paper leaves unsolved.

Reference: Shatnawi, F. O. K., Al-Maamari, A. A., Al-Qudah, Y. A., Amayreh, R. A. M., & Hulaiel, M. A. M. (2026). Judicial departure and legal certainty: a comparative study of US and French law. *Hasanuddin Law Review*, 12(1), 51–68.

Confidence: medium. Metadata is clean and the journal is DOAJ-indexed, but it is a modest venue and the finding is comparative-legal rather than formal — it strengthens an existing analogy rather than establishing a result.
