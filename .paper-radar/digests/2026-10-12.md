### Speciation paper — Section 4, The Ring-Species Problem: Local Compatibility, Global Incompatibility

**Speciation by local adaptation and isolation by distance in extended environments** — Hissa, L. D., de Aguiar, M. A. M., & Marquitti, F. M. D. (2026), *Evolution*, qpag179
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpag179) · verified via OpenAlex and Crossref (title, three authors, *Evolution*, issued 2026-10-05)

Bears on: the section's closing claim that the ring-species result — local pairwise compatibility accumulated step by step does not imply global compatibility between endpoints — is "a testable, falsifiable-in-simulation claim rather than a metaphor", requiring a first-order network simulator the series does not yet supply. The paper asserts the test is possible without naming a model class that performs it.

> The model class that test requires already exists. Hissa et al. (2026) simulate speciation in spatially extended environments under mating restricted by both spatial proximity and genetic similarity — formally the same pairing rule as K reconciliation between topologically adjacent, interpretively similar nodes — and find that environmental selection is not always necessary for reproductive isolation, which arises from restricted mating and limited dispersal alone, while two distinct environmental optima can accelerate diversification and reshape the timing of lineage splits. For the lineage network this fixes the division of labour between this section and Section 2: network distance is sufficient on its own to produce incompatible endpoints, and heterogeneous local adaptation sets how fast it happens rather than whether it happens.

Reference added: Hissa, L. D., de Aguiar, M. A. M., & Marquitti, F. M. D. (2026). Speciation by local adaptation and isolation by distance in extended environments. *Evolution*, qpag179.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 3, DNA as the Physical Substrate

**High-throughput in vivo DNA data encoding by multiplexed conjugation and orthogonal base editing** — Funke, L. F. H., Lin, Z., Liao, W., Ji, Y., & Poh, C. L. (2026), bioRxiv
[doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.08.757515) · verified via OpenAlex (title, first three authors, bioRxiv preprint, 2026)

Bears on: the Renewability requirement — that the probe must read, validate, repair and re-synthesize its archive — together with the paper's own concession that the literature behind renewability is thinner than that behind density and durability, molecular storage having developed almost entirely as passive cold archiving.

> One active write route has now been demonstrated without chemical synthesis at all: coupling multiplexed bacterial conjugation to genomic base editing, 45,689 bits were written into living-cell genomes and recovered at 100 percent and 99.5 percent accuracy with no computational error correction, with redundancy encoding improving recovery (Funke et al. 2026). Caveat: unreviewed preprint, and "biology capability yes, release no" means an in-vivo write path needs the governance paper's posture checked before it is leaned on.

### Governed-amendment paper — Section 8, corrigibility and a stand-in principal

**The Pushback Paradox: A Two-Probe Diagnostic for Language Model Compliance** — Bühler, S., Exler, D., Reischl, M., & Schutera, M. (2026), arXiv:2610.06673
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06673) · verified via arXiv API (title, first three authors, submitted 2026-10-05)

Bears on: the claim that corrigibility (Soares et al. 2015) "is defined relative to a principal whose corrections are authoritative, and the probe's principal is gone", so substituting or learning a principal relocates rather than dissolves the problem.

> The tension is measurable, not merely conceptual. Bühler et al. (2026) probe twelve language models with two instructions — act and accept a lower payoff, or wait and give up a higher one — reading the first as exploitability and the second as stoppability, and find that most of the models score alike on both and justify what they did by pointing to the instruction rather than to who issued it; none was exploitable but unstoppable, and only two could be stopped without being exploitable. A probe whose principal is gone inherits the unfavourable half of that result: a descendant corrigible enough to accept a correction is deferring to the form of an instruction, not to an entitlement to give one, which is precisely the surface the governance paper's hostile external agent would address.

### Lineage-network paper — Section 8, Byzantine tolerance at network scale

**Resilient Quorum Consensus over Disruption-Tolerant Multi-Transport Networks for Distributed Acoustic Sensing** — El Gemayel, C., El Gemayel, J., & Constantin, J. (2026), *Network*, 6(4), 86
[doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/network6040086) · verified via Crossref (title, three authors, MDPI *Network* 6(4):86, published 2026-10-07); abstract independently checked

Bears on: "Classical Byzantine fault-tolerant consensus requires synchronous rounds impossible at interstellar latencies."

> The point holds with greater force for the weaker crash-fault case. El Gemayel, El Gemayel & Constantin (2026) run Raft — a quorum protocol that assumes no malicious node at all — over a store-and-forward disruption-tolerant transport and find that safety survives partition: committed logs stay consistent, isolated leaders discard uncommitted entries on rejoining, and the system reconverges within a bounded recovery time once connectivity returns. What does not survive is availability during partition. A protocol that cannot make progress through a simulated partition has nothing to offer a network whose partitions last millennia, which is why the lineage buys agreement through independent confirmation and post hoc ledger audit rather than through rounds.
