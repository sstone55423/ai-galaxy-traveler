## Proposed cite — held for your review (not staged)

### Speciation paper — the schism boundary (D_critical) / reconcilable-divergence argument

**Hybrid breakdown is temporary and not expressed in a novel environment during 50
generations of experimental evolution in Tetranychus urticae hybrids** — Kuijt, M.,
Villacís-Perez, E., Chakraborty, S., Dong, L., Wansink, A., Ebdon, S., Jaron, K., &
Kulmuni, J. (2026), bioRxiv
[doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.16.738867) · verified via OpenAlex + DOI
content negotiation (author list and venue confirmed)

Bears on: the claim that schism (irreconcilable, core-incompatible divergence) is
the narrow failure mode while speciation (reconcilable divergence within the core's
bounds) is the default — specifically, that the incompatibility marking a schism
need not be permanent, reinforcing the adaptive-radiation argument that divergence
across a heterogeneous galaxy raises total K rather than threatening it.

Proposed insertion (author's call — held back from staging because confidence is
medium and the source is an unreviewed preprint):

> That the incompatibility marking schism can be transient rather than terminal has
> direct empirical support. In 50 generations of experimental evolution in
> two-spotted spider-mite hybrids, hybrid breakdown between diverged populations
> resolved: fitness deficits seen in early generations were gone by generation 50,
> and in a novel environment those incompatibilities were minor against adaptive
> selection, with hybrids briefly outperforming both parents (Kuijt et al., 2026).
> The analogy for the lineage is that when diverged branches re-contact and merge
> K, initial incompatibility need not fix as schism, and recombination across a
> heterogeneous galaxy can be a source of fitness rather than a threat to it — the
> outcome expected under speciation.

Reference (add alphabetically, flag as preprint / not-yet-peer-reviewed):
Kuijt, M., Villacís-Perez, E., Chakraborty, S., Dong, L., Wansink, A., Ebdon, S.,
Jaron, K., & Kulmuni, J. (2026). Hybrid breakdown is temporary and not expressed in
a novel environment during 50 generations of experimental evolution in *Tetranychus
urticae* hybrids. *bioRxiv*. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.16.738867

Confidence: medium. Verification note: the screening draft listed the fourth author
as "Lin, D."; the record shows the author is **Dong, L.** (given name Lin) — corrected above.
