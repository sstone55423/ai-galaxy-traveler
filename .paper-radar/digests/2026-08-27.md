### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 3, DNA as the Physical Substrate, after the Renewability paragraph

**Dynamic Operations in Macromolecular Data Storage** — Ossowski, J., Affenberger, O.,
Lutz, J.-F., & Nerantzaki, M. (2026), *Angewandte Chemie International Edition*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.5128253) · verified via OpenAlex (authors, year,
venue and publication date 2026-08-24 all confirmed; abstract retrieved and checked
against every claim made below)

Bears on: the Renewability ground in Section 3 — "a probe that can synthesize and
sequence DNA in situ ... can periodically read, validate, repair, and re-synthesize
the archive" — which is the property the whole architecture calls decisive, and the
only one of the three grounds that carried no supporting citation. Density has
Erlich & Zielinski (2017), Organick et al. (2018) and Wei et al. (2026); durability
has Allentoft et al. (2012) and Grass et al. (2015); renewability had none. This
minireview is the first support for it, and it supports the claim by showing how
little has been demonstrated.

> The literature behind the renewability claim is thinner than that behind density
> and durability. Molecular data storage has developed almost entirely as passive
> cold archiving, with the macromolecules serving as static archives; the
> reconfigurable operations renewability assumes — erasing, rewriting, repairing,
> and sorting encoded strands — have only recently been surveyed as a research
> direction in their own right, across both DNA and synthetic sequence-defined
> polymers (Ossowski et al. 2026). Renewability is therefore the property this
> architecture leans on hardest and the one least demonstrated: a design requirement
> placed on the probe's chemistry, not a capability inherited from the terrestrial
> state of the art.

Reference added: Ossowski, J., Affenberger, O., Lutz, J.-F., & Nerantzaki, M. (2026).
Dynamic operations in macromolecular data storage. *Angewandte Chemie International
Edition*.

Confidence: high. It is a minireview rather than a primary result, and the prose says
so — it is cited for the state of the field, not for a demonstrated capability.

- **Instrumental succession: the gradual transfer of agency to AI** — Estep, P. W.
  (2026), *Frontiers in Psychology* [doi](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1922407)
  · verified via OpenAlex
  Disputes the Omohundro (2008) / Bostrom (2014) instrumental-convergence result the
  payload paper leans on in Section 2 to motivate the immutable core — that a
  sufficiently capable system acquires resistance to having its goals changed. Estep
  argues frontier LLM-class systems have no primary terminal goal and so generate no
  such drives, relocating the risk to gradual human handoff of agency. The critique
  is scoped to systems that serve as conduits for transient user goals; the probe is
  by construction the opposite case, so the payload paper's claim survives it. Worth
  a qualifying clause on the Omohundro/Bostrom appeal, not an argument change.
