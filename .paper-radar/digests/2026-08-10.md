## Staged

### Payload paper — §3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting (high confidence)

**When Memory Becomes Authority: Benchmarking Authority Collapse at the Memory
Consolidation Boundary** — Zhan, Q., Zhang, R., Guo, S., Zhao, L., & Liu, Z.
(2026), arXiv:2608.01679 · [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01679) · verified
(v2, 2026-08-04; all five authors and all quoted figures confirmed against the
arXiv record/abstract).

Bears on: §3's claim that the integrity ledger secures syntactic integrity and
provenance only — that an unbroken hash chain does not guarantee decisions
follow authorized procedures. This grounds the *procedural* failure level,
which the paper names but does not anchor in any mechanism.

Staged insertion (after the threat-taxonomy paragraph):

> Recent empirical work on agent memory gives the procedural level a concrete
> mechanism. In agents that consolidate accumulated history into reusable
> facts, observations, and standing rules, consolidation routinely preserves a
> claim while erasing the source constraints that governed its authorized use —
> authority collapse, observed in 48 of 49 tested configurations, with
> collapsed memories producing unauthorized actions at a mean rate of 50.3%
> until authority labels were persisted alongside the claim (Zhan et al.,
> 2026). The probe's archive meets the same boundary at far longer range: a
> hash chain that proves a claim unaltered says nothing about whether the
> authority under which it was admitted still licenses acting on it, so the
> ledger must record not only what was learned but the authority under which
> it may be reused.

## Proposed (not staged)

### DNA mission-ledger paper — §5, The Mission Ledger (medium confidence)

**MutMem: Cryptographically Authorized Mutation in Persistent Agent Memory** —
Saidi, W. (2026), arXiv:2608.02843 ·
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.02843) · verified.

Bears on: §5's claim that authorized adaptation of a mutable memory can be
mechanically distinguished from tampering by signed, hash-linked transitions,
and §9's caveat that the ledger proves a change happened and by whom, not that
it was wise. Proposed insertion:

> The mechanism is not merely notional. Work on persistent memory for
> terrestrial AI agents implements the same distinction — authorized adaptation
> versus tampering — by committing every nontrivial change as a signed
> transition binding its provenance node, signer epoch, old and new values, a
> hash-linked predecessor, and SHA-256 commitments, with suspect content
> retained under signed, revisable labels rather than silently deleted (Saidi,
> 2026). That work also illustrates the limit stated in Section 9: the chain
> establishes that a change was authorized and by whom, not that it was
> correct.

Held at medium: unreviewed single-author preprint with self-reported
benchmarks. Safe as an existence-proof of the mechanism; if the reference list
should stay anchored to peer-reviewed foundations (Merkle, Lamport, Haber &
Stornetta), skipping it is defensible.

### Speciation paper — §8, The Core-Intact / Cognitive-Divergence Frame (medium confidence)

**When Self-Evolution Backfires: Pre-Commit Gating against Skill Contamination
in LLM Agents** — Shang, L., Xu, M., Sun, Y., Xia, T., Hu, L., Xu, L., &
Zheng, N. (2026), arXiv:2608.05810 ·
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05810) · verified (note: seven authors; the
screening pass listed six — Zheng added from the arXiv record).

Bears on: §8's recommendation of "a monitoring strategy rather than a
suppression strategy," and §5's D_critical, which posits a permanence threshold
without an empirical case. The study finds capability accumulation in
self-evolving agents is non-monotonic past a critical pool size and the
degradation structurally irreversible — an analogue supporting D_critical
while cutting against monitoring-only. Proposed insertion:

> A small-scale empirical analogue cuts against monitoring alone. Shang et al.
> (2026) find that capability accumulation in self-evolving LLM agents is
> non-monotonic — past a critical pool size, newly distilled skills degrade
> performance rather than improving it — and that the degradation is
> structurally irreversible, because a defective skill becomes reference
> material for later ones and removing it after the fact recovers only a small
> fraction of the lost performance. If path-dependent self-modification behaves
> this way at lineage timescales, detection after divergence has propagated is
> not equivalent to prevention, and the admission-time gating their result
> argues for has no counterpart in a monitoring layer that checks only the
> core hash and the replication policy.

Held at medium: the domain is LLM agent skill pools on a software benchmark,
not deep-time lineages; worded as an analogue, but worth a read before
inserting.
