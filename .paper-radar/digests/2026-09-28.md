### Payload paper — Section 3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting

**Self-Healing Harness for Runtime Oversight of Agent Self-Modification** — Tayebati, S., Kumar, D., Darabi, N., Krishnan, R., & Trivedi, A. R. (2026), arXiv:2609.24130
[link](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.24130) · verified via OpenAlex and DataCite (title, year, all five authors, arXiv as publisher)

Bears on: the claim that the interpretive layer is "held to far higher validation thresholds than ordinary learning" — the paper asserts the threshold but never says what it tests — and on the Schmidhuber (2007) proof-before-adoption framing in Section 3.

> Tayebati et al. (2026) supply the engineering form of that proof requirement, and a warning about its cost: an agent proposes changes to its own operating instructions, and an external runtime gate grants them provisional authority during evaluation but persistent cross-episode authority only after measured improvement on the triggering failure without regression beyond a fixed margin on a set of protected cases. Of 383 proposals the gate decided by replay, 211 — 55% — repaired the failure that prompted them while breaking a case that had previously worked, and task-completion score was higher with the gate than without it in all sixteen matched runs across three agent benchmarks. The horizon is a benchmark episode rather than a megayear, but the result names what the interpretive layer's higher validation threshold must actually test: local proof of benefit is not proof of benefit, and a self-modification is admissible only against a protected set the change was not allowed to touch.

Reference added: Tayebati, S., Kumar, D., Darabi, N., Krishnan, R., & Trivedi, A. R. (2026). Self-healing harness for runtime oversight of agent self-modification. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2609.24130.

Confidence: high. Every figure is stated in the abstract. The abstract does not report the paired bootstrap intervals in full, so the prose claims no statistical significance — do not strengthen it on redraft.

### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 9, Network Multiplication: Why K_net Is Super-Additive

**Divergent strategies and convergent outcomes in autonomous materials discovery** — Kim, J. (2026), arXiv:2609.23957
[link](https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.23957) · verified via OpenAlex, DataCite and the arXiv API (single author confirmed — the screener flagged the single-author record as possibly truncated metadata; it is not)

Bears on: the "Independent detections raise confidence non-linearly" claim, and the same independence premise where it recurs in the Section 9 quarantine pipeline ("independent confirmation from at least one additional node") and in Section 12's c_j arithmetic. A lineage is the worst case for that premise: every node inherits the same core, the same K_0 archive, and one architecture.

> The independence that argument rests on is weaker for a lineage than for two unrelated observatories. Sixteen separately initialized agent sessions of one model-harness configuration, given the same frozen database and the same objective, diverged widely in strategy — 100 to 5,000 structures screened — yet converged on the same result, and fifteen of the sixteen selected the same corrupted database entry, an incomplete structure whose missing anions created an artificial pore volume (Kim 2026). Enforced procedural checks raised fresh-run reproduction from one of eight to eight of eight without detectably improving the validity of the conclusions: shared inputs and a shared architecture produce common-mode errors that agreement between nodes cannot surface, so the confidence gain from a second detection should be weighted by how much of the detecting pipeline the two nodes do not share.

Reference added: Kim, J. (2026). Divergent strategies and convergent outcomes in autonomous materials discovery. *arXiv preprint arXiv:2609.23957*.

Confidence: high. No existing Kim reference in this paper, so the in-text form is unambiguous.

### Ethics paper — Section 10, Procedural Legitimacy: When Verification Becomes Adjudication

**Office and unowned architecture: public judgment in the age of algorithmic governance** — Wong, S.-M. (2026), *Ethics and Information Technology*, 28(4)
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10676-026-09919-9) · verified via OpenAlex and Crossref

The strongest of the unstaged set. Wong reads Kant's *Rechtslehre* to argue that a determination which is materially burdensome, legally operative, reasonably contestable and practically final must have its operative ground attributable to an answerable office, and names a threshold at which algorithmic assistance becomes impermissible substitution. All five mechanisms Section 10 catalogues meet his four conditions and none has an office behind it: across one-way delays of years to a century there is no forum and no authority that could adopt a quarantine or a kin-check as its own judgment. That makes the section's four-item checklist the most this architecture can offer rather than a discharge of the demand. Transfer from a state determining a person's legal status to probes adjudicating one another is the paper's inference, as it already is with Fuller (1964) and Citron (2008).

Note: Crossref gives the author as Sai-Ming Wong, OpenAlex as Simon Sai-ming Wong. Use Wong, S.-M. and confirm against the published PDF before it lands.

### Routing paper — Section 7, Robustness to Node Failure

**Impact of seed node position on network robustness under localized attacks** — Chujyo, M., Liu, S., & Toriumi, F. (2026), *PLOS ONE*, 21(9), e0358769
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0358769) · verified via Crossref (journal version) — OpenAlex resolves this DOI to the arXiv preprint record, so the venue was checked separately and confirmed

A third failure geometry beside the paper's random loss and the security paper's targeted cut-vertex attack: a failure that propagates outward from one node along the edges it settled. Damage depends strongly on where it starts, and degree, closeness and betweenness all predict robustness loss worse than an index built from the links severed as it spreads. If it holds, the random-removal floor is not conservative for correlated loss and the cut-vertex ranking is not the right priority list against it.

Medium because the physical bridge is yours to accept: settlements light-years apart have no natural neighbour-to-neighbour cascade, so this geometry only earns its place under the security paper's hostile-lineage or autoimmunity mechanism. Volume, issue and page above come from Crossref, not from the candidate record.

## Screener notes on query precision

Three screeners independently reported the same two problems, both cheap to fix in `queries.json`:

- **Crossref noise from overloaded life-science terms.** "Replication", "probe", "speciation" and "contact" are pulling virology, lab reagents, chemistry speciation analysis and sports medicine in volume. The `computational` query returned exactly one candidate and it was a dengue paper.
- **The `security` query recalls on bare "robustness" and "network"**, which pulls clinical network meta-analysis and photogrammetry registration. Constraining it with *articulation point*, *percolation*, *targeted node removal*, *blast radius* or *containment* would raise precision sharply.

Separately: the Research Square feed appears to drop abstracts systematically — at least eight candidates this week arrived with an empty `abstract` field, including the two most on-thesis items in the whole sweep (interplanetary time attestation, DNA-FRSC). Backfilling abstracts from Crossref before the screening step would have turned several `consider` verdicts into definite ones. Three eLife peer-review stubs (`...sa1/sa2/sa3`) also duplicate their parent article's abstract and could be filtered by DOI suffix.
