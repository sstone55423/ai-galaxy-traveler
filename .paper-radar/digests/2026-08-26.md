### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 2, Defining K, after the King (2026) paragraph

**Remaking the landscape of scientific expertise: The emergent research program of
total automation in science** — Peterson, D., Koch, B., Natarajan, R., & Panofsky, A.
(2026), *Big Data & Society*, 13(3)
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1177/20539517261473358) · verified via OpenAlex and Crossref
(authors, year, venue, volume/issue all confirmed)

Bears on: the claim that the observation-to-validated-claim pipeline, not archive
size, is what converts data to K — and on King (2026)'s framing that the open
problem is integrating the automated steps of science into one unsupervised agent.
Peterson et al. name which competencies must be integrated, and identify a
precondition the probe cannot satisfy: automation to date has advanced partly by
reshaping scientific practice to suit machines, which is not an option at the far
end of a transit.

> Peterson et al. (2026) interviewed 26 DARPA contractors, program managers and
> others involved in scientific automation, and find that these efforts decompose
> scientific expertise into three targets — insight (hypothesis generation and
> pattern recognition), embodied knowledge (experimental procedures and techniques),
> and judgment (evaluation of scientific claims) — and that progress comes
> substantially from reshaping scientific practice to suit machines, through
> standardized ontologies and machine-readable data formats, rather than from
> replicating human capability. The probe can carry that reshaping inward, into its
> own archive and K packet structure, but not outward: nothing at the far end of the
> transit will be formatted for it. What the probe must supply unaided is judgment,
> and judgment is what the confidence term c_j carries in the metric defined below.

Reference added: Peterson, D., Koch, B., Natarajan, R., & Panofsky, A. (2026).
Remaking the landscape of scientific expertise: The emergent research program of
total automation in science. *Big Data & Society*, 13(3).

## Flagged (no insertion proposed)

- **Missing physics discovery through fully differentiable finite element-based
  machine learning** — Farsi, A., Bouziani, N., & Ham, D. A. (2026),
  *Scientific Reports* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-65125-z) · verified
  via Crossref (no volume assigned yet)
  A learned constitutive operator that is independent of system configuration and so
  transfers zero-shot to new geometries and boundary conditions. That is the shape of
  the operational-K transfer the series assumes when a settlement's solved failure
  mode propagates as meta-knowledge to a child probe in a different system
  (knowledge-growth paper, Sections 5 and 12). Computational mechanics with no
  deep-time or autonomy content, so no specific claim to attach it to yet.

- **Driverless, not thoughtless? Automated vehicle regulation, the dilemma of
  control, and the prospect of dual corrigibility** — Haugland, B. T. (2026),
  *Technological Forecasting and Social Change*, 233
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.techfore.2026.124866) · verified via Crossref
  Screened on title only — no abstract was retrievable. Pairs the Collingridge
  dilemma with "dual corrigibility", which structurally resembles the
  governed-amendment paper's problem: the core is entrenched when the lineage knows
  least, and the lineage that later knows enough to amend it is the one the core
  exists to constrain. Caution: "corrigibility" in an STS venue is likely the
  legal-sociological sense, not the alignment sense the paper uses. Read the abstract
  before citing.

- **A Theoretical Sim-to-Real Transfer Framework for Deep Reinforcement Learning in
  Autonomous Spacecraft Attitude Control** — Elfarran, M. (2026), engrXiv preprint
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.31224/8059) · verified via Crossref (posted content,
  Open Engineering Inc; not peer reviewed)
  Its "Safety and Certification Envelope" plus bounded post-deployment adaptation is
  the payload paper's immutable-core-constraining-a-self-modifying-layer move in a
  narrow domain. Redundant against the shielded-RL citation already in Section 3, and
  it reports no results. The one new angle is *bounded parameter drift* as distinct

- **Song divergence and a gleaming white iris reveal four species in a widespread
  Neotropical understory bird (*Clibanornis rubiginosus*, Furnariidae)** —
  Villamizar, J. C., & Cuervo, A. M. (2026), bioRxiv preprint
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.24.746896) · verified via Crossref
  (not peer reviewed)

  incompatibility measure. A single-genus taxonomic revision, so it supports no
  specific claim on its own.
