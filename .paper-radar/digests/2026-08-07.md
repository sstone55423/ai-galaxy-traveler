[doi](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003488071-6) · verified via Crossref (title, authors, year, venue, pages)

Bears on: the claim that the constitutional-amendment analogy is instructive about
form but imports an enforcement context the probe lacks, and Section 10's point that
captured amendment is hard to catch because every step is procedurally valid.

Proposed insertion (Section 8, after the sentence on the enforcement context the probe lacks):

> The human record supplies the failure directly. Albert's category of constitutional
> dismemberment names a change that follows the amendment procedure while substantively
> replacing the constitutional identity the procedure exists to protect, and the Cuban
> reforms of 1992 and 2002 are read as exactly that — formally partial amendments within
> the 1976 Constitution that transformed state ideology, economic structure, and citizen
> participation, in the absence of effective constitutional control mechanisms
> (Fernández Estrada & Fernández Pérez 2026). That is the captured-amendment mode of
> Section 10 in its human instance, and it occurred in a polity that had courts, a
> continuous deliberating body, and no message delay at all — none of which the lineage
> will have.

If adopted, pair with Albert (2019), *Constitutional Amendments: Making, Breaking, and
Changing Constitutions* (Oxford), the source of the dismemberment theory this chapter applies.
