### Payload paper — Section 3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting

**Meta^n: Recursive Self-Improvement through Emergent Depth** — Kim, Z. M.,
Lee, Y.-J., Jwa, S., & Kang, D. (2026), arXiv:2608.24735
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24735) · verified via arXiv API

Bears on: the claim that stability is bought structurally — by a small invariant
held outside the learner — rather than by constraining the cognitive layer's
capacity to change.

> Recent work on recursive self-improvement supports the structural form of that
> answer. Kim et al. (2026) observe that systems which edit themselves must leave
> part of their own editing machinery untouched to stay stable, capping the
> meta-depth they realize at roughly two, and respond by holding the
> meta-operation fixed and recursing on its input instead: because the operation
> never changes it cannot destabilize the system, and depth is set by convergence
> rather than fixed in advance. That is the immutable core's logic in miniature —
> the one component never improved is what lets everything below it improve
> without a fixed ceiling — demonstrated over benchmark runs rather than megayears.

Reference added: Kim, Z. M., Lee, Y.-J., Jwa, S., & Kang, D. (2026). Meta^n:
Recursive self-improvement through emergent depth. *arXiv preprint*
arXiv:2608.24735.

Confidence: high.

### Payload paper — Section 3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting

**Reward-Free Continual Adaptation for Resilient Space Robots** — Orsula, A.,
Olivares-Mendez, M., & Martinez, C. (2026), arXiv:2608.23452
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23452) · verified via arXiv API

Bears on: the germline/soma partition — an immutable value core with a freely
self-modifying cognitive layer — and the accompanying claim that spacecraft
autonomy already implements a weak version of enforcing constraints outside the
learner.

> The complementary move, freezing what encodes value while adapting what models
> the world, has been demonstrated on the probe's own class of problem. Orsula,
> Olivares-Mendez and Martinez (2026) address space robots suffering severe
> hardware degradation where no reward signal is available at deployment,
> freezing a pretrained world model's observation encoder and reward predictor
> while updating only its transition dynamics through unsupervised rollouts, and
> retraining the policy entirely on imagined trajectories. The tasks are
> planetary traversal, orbital navigation, and precision assembly rather than
> megayear self-repair, but the partition is the one proposed here: what the
> agent is for is held fixed while its model of what it is and where it is stays
> free to change.

Reference added: Orsula, A., Olivares-Mendez, M., & Martinez, C. (2026).
Reward-free continual adaptation for resilient space robots. *arXiv preprint*
arXiv:2608.23452.

Confidence: high.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 3, DNA as the Physical Substrate (Density)

**Towards density-optimal DNA storage with practical bijective coding** —
Li, J., Dong, M., Qi, Z., & Guan, H. (2026), *Nature Communications*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77024-y) · verified via OpenAlex

Bears on: the density paragraph's argument that the Erlich & Zielinski
2 × 10¹⁷ B/g figure is an upper bound any downstream use must derate. The new
citation names copy number as the dominant derating term, which the paragraph's
existing three factors (encapsulation, coding overhead, primer count) did not
separate out.

> Codec design continues to move the raw figure: a bijective code that maps
> binary data onto nearly all suitable low-error sequences reaches 1.66 bits per
> nucleotide and a capacity of 41 exabytes per gram, with perfect retrieval at
> six copies per strand (Li et al. 2026). Most of the gap between that number and
> the figure above is copy number, which is precisely the derating this section
> insists on — an archive built for megayear survival sits at far higher
> redundancy than six copies, so its delivered density falls well below any
> headline quoted at minimal copy number.

Reference added: Li, J., Dong, M., Qi, Z., & Guan, H. (2026). Towards
density-optimal DNA storage with practical bijective coding. *Nature
Communications*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-77024-y

Confidence: high.

### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 9, Network Multiplication: Why K_net Is Super-Additive

**Autonomous Mathematical Discovery in an Open-World Multi-Agent Environment** —
Chung, S., Du, W., & Wesley, W. J. (2026), arXiv:2608.23691
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23691) · verified via arXiv API

Bears on: the "Lamarckian propagation multiplies operational K" claim, which is
currently asserted from first principles with no empirical existence proof —
unlike the self-improvement claims in Section 5, which carry citations.

> Super-additivity from a shared record has a narrow existence proof at machine
> speed. In the Station, an open-world environment where agents drawn from
> different model families pursue a shared research goal with no central
> coordinator and no scripted pipeline, the agents chose their own directions and
> built a shared literature; across 12 construction problems from the AlphaEvolve
> catalogue and two further case studies they obtained results novel relative to
> the prior literature on five, including a new infinite family of finite-field
> Kakeya sets and a substantially improved lower bound for Erdős's
> minimum-overlap problem (Chung, Du & Wesley 2026). Two features transfer
> directly — heterogeneous cognition, whose lineage analogue is interpretive
> drift between branches, and coordination through a written record rather than a
> controller, which is what the network layer supplies. The disanalogy is
> latency: the Station's agents read each other within hours, where lineage nodes
> exchange K packets across centuries.

Reference added: Chung, S., Du, W., & Wesley, W. J. (2026). Autonomous
mathematical discovery in an open-world multi-agent environment. *arXiv preprint*
arXiv:2608.23691.

Confidence: high.

- **HORIZON: A Read-Efficient Firmware for DNA Storage with Horizontal Layout** —
  Sensintaffar, A., Kiran, R., Chen, Y., Zheng, M., & Li, B. (2026),
  arXiv:2608.24839 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24839) · verified
  *DNA mission-ledger paper, Section 8.* Random access to a DNA pool is by primer
  pair, so a vault sharing primer pools with the working archive is pulled on
  every refresh cycle — the opposite of the "read rarely" condition its
  durability argument assumes. Argues the three storage regimes must be separated
  physically, not only logically.

- **Safety Does Not Compose: Non-Decaying Loop State for Autonomous LLM Agents** —
  Wu, C., Jia, H., Liu, Y., Yang, Y., et al. (2026), arXiv:2608.27141
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27141) · verified
  *Governed-amendment paper, Section 10.* Claims a separation result: against a
  threat whose evidence is fragmented across iterations, a monitor scoped to a
  single trajectory has true-positive rate equal to its false-positive rate. Read
  across generations, this says a lineage validating each amendment on its own
  merits cannot in principle detect the amendment ratchet — which would make the
  Section 7 requirement to preserve the founding core as an auditable baseline
  load-bearing rather than hygienic. Worth reading before staging; the transfer
  from iterations to generations is the author's call.

- **BixBench3: Benchmarking AI agents on research-study-scale computational
  biology tasks** — Koch, Z., Wassie, A. T., Valdés-Alemán, J., et al. (2026),
  arXiv:2608.25286 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25286) · verified
  *Knowledge-growth paper, Section 5.* Best of 13 frontier models reproduced 48%
  of study artifacts; scores fell from 0.36 on one- to two-step analyses to 0.24
  on longer chains, and from 0.36 to 0.10 as raw data crossed 100 GB. Both
  gradients run the wrong way for a probe whose science program is deeply
  sequential and whose archive is large — a measured degradation where the
  section currently carries a survey-level assertion.

- **Giant strongly biconnected components of directed networks** — Yang, M.,
  Laubenbacher, R., & Min, B. (2026), arXiv:2608.25562
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.25562) · verified
  *Routing paper, Section 3.* The giant biconnected component appears at the same
  percolation threshold as the giant connected component but grows more slowly,
  so reachability figures are an upper bound on durable coverage. Formal
  complement to the articulation-point analysis, and the routing paper currently
  cites no network-science robustness literature at all. Caveat: derived for
  directed networks, while the stellar reachability graph at fixed hop distance is
  undirected — the transfer is by analogy.
