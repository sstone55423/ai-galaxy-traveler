## Flagged (no insertion staged)

- **Maintenance of phenotypic divergence in two sympatric monkeyflowers with
  weak reproductive isolation** — Arenas-Castro, H., Cochran, C., Evans, Q.,
  Chen, H., & Coughlan, J. M. (2026), *bioRxiv* preprint, posted 2026-08-01
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.07.28.741377) · verified via the bioRxiv
  API (title, five authors, DOI, date, category all match; **not peer-reviewed**)

  Bears on the speciation paper, Section 6 (horizontal transfer and the limits
  of the tree model), and specifically on the claim that if lateral K-exchange
  becomes routine between historically unconnected branches, that exchange is
  the mechanism preventing Section 4's ring-species outcome from becoming
  permanent. The empirical record here runs the other way: two sympatric
  Mimulus species with minimal reproductive isolation and extensive gene flow
  stay phenotypically distinct at F_ST = 0.0335, with more than half of the
  highly differentiated loci co-localizing with either trait QTLs or putative
  structural variants (rarely both), and a previously characterized inversion
  associated with life-history divergence enriched for those loci. The transfer
  to the lineage is qualitative: high-volume K exchange between branches is not
  by itself reconciliation. If a branch's adaptations are carried by tightly
  coupled design complexes that are only useful as a set, D(t) can keep growing
  across an open channel.

  Not staged: bioRxiv v1 with no journal version, and the screening confidence
  was medium. It constrains Section 6 qualitatively; it does not speak to the
  Section 5 drift clock, which measures rates the paper does not report. Worth
  reading before the next speciation revision, and worth re-checking in a few
  months for a peer-reviewed version.
