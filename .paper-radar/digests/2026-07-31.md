## Proposed (medium confidence — not staged)

### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 5, "Cruise-Phase Operational K: AI Self-Improvement"

**Learning on the Job: Continual Learning from Deployment Feedback for Frozen-Weights Agents** — Tablan, V., Taylor, S., & Bernhem, K. (2026), arXiv:2607.22157 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.22157) · verified via arXiv API (title, all three authors, 2026-07-24 submission, cs.AI all match)

Bears on: the claim that the probe's own learning, fault-detection and planning algorithms are refined over decades and centuries of operation and that those refinements are retained as meta-knowledge — and, in Section 12, that complementary K acquired along different paths adds directly on probe-probe encounter.

> Operational K need not be carried in the weights. Work on frozen-weight agents shows that ordinary operation supplies enough signal for continual improvement when each episode is distilled into an external store of retrievable rules: on a service-task benchmark, learning from a one-bit outcome verdict raised single-trial success to 1.6 times a static-retrieval baseline and learning from after-the-fact corrections to 2.6 times, solving 22 of 84 tasks the baseline never solved (Tablan et al., 2026). The same study found that the accumulated store transfers between different underlying models, each rising above its own no-memory baseline — a small-scale analogue of the complementary-K exchange of Section 12, and evidence that operational K can accumulate, and move between probes, without modifying the cognitive substrate that produced it.

Reference: Tablan, V., Taylor, S., & Bernhem, K. (2026). Learning on the job: Continual learning from deployment feedback for frozen-weights agents. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2607.22157.

Confidence: medium. Every number quoted (1.6x, 2.6x, 22 of 84) is stated in the abstract and was checked against it. The weakness is scale: a banking-domain benchmark over single episodes, not centuries of autonomous operation. The transfer result is the more interesting half for this series and is the part the insertion leans on.

### Payload paper — Section 5, "From Data to Knowledge"

**Ground Truth First: A Longitudinal Evaluation Instrument for Agent Memory, and the Tenure Crossover in Memory-Architecture Rankings** — Spencer, Q. (2026), arXiv:2607.21962 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.21962) · verified via arXiv API (title, sole author, 2026-07-24 submission, cs.CL all match)

Bears on: the tiered-retention scheme — permanent core and rare observations, compressed bulk, only routine low-value streams discarded — and the implicit assumption that a value-weighted triage policy chosen at design time stays the right one across the mission. Also reinforces Section 8's admission that knowledge triage is an unformalized optimization.

> A caution is owed here from the little empirical evidence that exists. In a longitudinal benchmark of agent memory architectures, backend rankings inverted with history length: a budgeted, aggressively curating memory led at a three-week horizon but fell from 96% to 72% recall of evicted content by nine weeks, while a provenance-typed graph rose to 90% (Spencer, 2026). The horizons are weeks and the agents are nothing like this payload, but the direction is the relevant result — eviction policies that look optimal over short histories degrade with tenure, and provenance-typed structure is what holds up — so the retention tiers above should be read as a starting policy the probe must be able to revisit, not a setting that can be validated once at launch.

Reference: Spencer, Q. (2026). Ground truth first: A longitudinal evaluation instrument for agent memory, and the tenure crossover in memory-architecture rankings. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2607.21962.

Confidence: medium. The 96%/72%/90% figures and the three- and nine-week horizons are all in the abstract. The corpus is synthetic and fictionalized by construction, which the author states as a design feature; the insertion does not claim more than a directional result.
