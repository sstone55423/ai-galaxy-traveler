### Ethics paper — Section 1, Introduction: The One-Way Door

**Artificial Externality: A Three-Layer Model of Reality from Substrate to Smart Contract** — Suzuki, K. (2026), *Philosophy & Technology*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-026-01158-6) · verified via OpenAlex (Keisuke Suzuki, 2026-08-11, *Philosophy & Technology*)

Bears on: the paragraph grounding the launch's irreversibility in Jonas (1984) — "acts whose consequences extend beyond the agent's ability to foresee, control, or correct." Suzuki supplies the distinction Jonas does not make: the lineage's irreversibility is engineered, not incidental. His central concept describes the immutable core exactly, and sharpens Section 7's value-imposition argument by naming what fixing the core actually does.

Appended to the existing paragraph:

> Jonas' concern was consequences that outrun foresight; the lineage adds a property he did not address, irreversibility that is engineered rather than incidental. Suzuki (2026) names this artificial externality: human-made structures whose resistance to intervention is deliberately built to be practically insurmountable even for their creators, and which thereby acquire a givenness once attributed only to nature. The immutable core is such a structure. The launch does not merely place the lineage beyond recall; it manufactures on purpose a constraint the launch team itself cannot later lift.

Reference added: Suzuki, K. (2026). Artificial externality: A three-layer model of reality from substrate to smart contract. *Philosophy & Technology*. https://doi.org/10.1007/s13347-026-01158-6

Confidence: high on metadata and on the concept match, which the abstract supports directly. Note that Suzuki's own cases are virtual environments, AI agents and smart contracts — the interstellar application is yours, not his, and the prose is written to claim only what he claims.

- **Early Design Exploration of Aerospace Systems Using Assume–Guarantee Contracts** — Rouquette, Pinto & Incer (2026), *Journal of Aerospace Information Systems*
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.2514/1.i011529) · verified via OpenAlex
  *Payload paper.* Assume–guarantee contracts abstract each component to what it assumes of its environment and what it guarantees in return, deriving system-level properties algebraically from local ones. That is structurally the problem the payload paper poses for the immutable core: a small fixed specification every mutable replacement must satisfy, with value stability derived compositionally rather than by inspecting a freely rewritten implementation. The abstract demonstrates only design-space exploration, not runtime self-modification, so nothing is citable without reading whether Pacti's refinement and quotient operations actually support that use. Possible formal vocabulary for the core / interpretive-layer boundary.

- **The AI Method and the Scientific Method: Epistemic Search, the Validation Paradox, and the Discovery–Validation Loop** — Matta, D. (2026), Zenodo preprint
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21868049) · verified via OpenAlex
  *Knowledge-growth paper.* The closest thing to a cite in the batch. Its validation paradox — AI lowers the cost of generating claims faster than the cost of warranting them — bears directly on K(t) = G(t) − L(t) and on the claim that cruise is an active K-growth phase. A probe in cruise generates hypotheses cheaply but can validate almost none of them, so unwarranted generation inflates G without raising K. Held back because it is an author-deposited preprint with no venue or review; a refereed source for the same point would be worth finding first.

- **The Limits of Weakened Stare Decisis: Cautionary Lessons from the United States for Australia** — Barnes, A. & Roberts, J. (2026), *The University of Queensland Law Journal*
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.38127/uqlj.17499) · verified via OpenAlex
  *Governed-amendment paper.* Examines when an apex court legitimately overturns its own constitutional interpretations, and argues that weakening the presumption against change becomes illegitimate when caused by — or perceived as — politicization of the deciding body. Maps onto the core / interpretive-layer split: the core's effective content can shift through reinterpretation rather than formal amendment, and the legitimacy of that route depends on the perceived impartiality of a body the lineage has no external check on. A source for "interpretive drift is a de facto amendment channel."

- **Topographic complexity shapes adaptive genomic variation in co-occurring plant species** — Lobos, Ahrens, Rymer, Hodgins & Miller (2026), bioRxiv preprint
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.07.743600) · verified via OpenAlex
  *Speciation paper.* One relevant result: adaptive variants persist at fine spatial scales despite low genome-wide divergence and ongoing gene flow — local selection can sustain divergence even in a connected population, which is the mechanism needed for divergence to accumulate between branches that stay in contact through the lineage network. A single reduced-representation SNP study in one Australian park; treat it as a pointer to the divergence-with-gene-flow literature rather than a citation.
