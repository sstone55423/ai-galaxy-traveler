- **Partitioned Memory Storage Inspired Few-Shot Class-Incremental Learning** —
  Yin, Y., Deng, W., Zhang, J., Zhang, J., Cheng, X., & Zhang, J. (2026),
  *Entropy* 28(8), 843
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28080843) · verified via OpenAlex
  Payload paper. Frames the stability–plasticity dilemma explicitly and uses
  per-session model partitioning rather than replay or regularization —
  structurally analogous to the immutable core / interpretive layer / mutable
  cognitive layer split. Evidence is CIFAR-100 and mini-ImageNet accuracy over
  short training sessions, so it supports nothing about retention over
  millennia or about value stability. No claim to attach it to.

  Eiffert, B. (2026), *Zenodo*

  Knowledge-growth paper. A working federated self-driving-laboratory ecosystem

  non-peer-reviewed lab report about software architecture, not about
  knowledge accumulation rate or loss, so it underwrites no numeric claim.

  through Learning, Decision-Making, and Diachronic Identity** — Yörük, M.
  (2026), *Zenodo* (preprint)
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.21708683) · verified via OpenAlex
  Ethics paper. Decouples individuality from consciousness, moral patiency, and
  legal personhood, with diachronic continuity as a criterion — the same move
  as the moral-status and legal-personhood sections, and a possible formal
  handle on the claim that the ten-thousandth-generation probe is not the same
  entity as the seed. Gunkel already does this work more authoritatively;
  adding a single-author preprint would duplicate rather than extend.

- **Secession referenda and the perception of judicial objectivity in Britain,
  Italy and Spain** — Breda, V. (2026), *Global Constitutionalism*
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.1017/s2045381726100422) · verified via OpenAlex
  Governed-amendment paper. Argues that a visibly impartial institutional
  setting is a precondition for any credible settlement of a secession dispute.
  That would sharpen the constitutional-analogy argument from the inside: human
  polities with functioning courts still struggle to supply a neutral arbiter,
  and a lineage has no court at all. The abstract's actual content is narrow
  (recusal standards and panel composition in three judgments), and the schism
  discussion is not currently framed around arbiter legitimacy.
