  Zheng, J., Fang, X., Zhang, J., Gui, Z., Chen, H., et al. (2026), arXiv:2608.05013
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.05013) · verified via arXiv API
  Payload paper. Its failure taxonomy for long-horizon autonomous agents — goal
  drift, state loss, context overflow — is the same list Section 3's two-layer
  architecture defends against, and it reports that bounded subtask decomposition
  plus managed execution memory helps across five LLM backends. The horizon is one
  day over 104 tasks, and the abstract supports nothing about value stability under
  self-modification or an immutable core, so a megayear-scale insertion would
  overreach the evidence. Useful only as ground-level evidence that goal
  preservation degrades with horizon length even at the scale of hours.

- **The Cuban Constitutional Reforms of 1992 and 2002 as Examples of
  Constitutional Dismemberment** — Fernández Estrada, J. A., Fernández Pérez, M.
  (2026), in *Comparative Constitutional Reform in Latin America*, Routledge
  [doi](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781003488071-6) · verified via Crossref
  Governed-amendment paper. Applies Albert's constitutional-dismemberment theory:
  reforms presented as partial amendments within the existing framework that
  substantively transformed constitutional identity while bypassing constituent
  power, then were legitimized retroactively. That is a real-world instance of
  three of Section 10's failure modes (captured amendment, amendment ratchet,
  emergency-policy creep) and of Section 8's claim that constitutional analogy
  secures form but not correctness. The single-jurisdiction case study is not the
  right citation; Albert's underlying amendment/dismemberment framework probably is,
  and would need to be found and verified separately.
