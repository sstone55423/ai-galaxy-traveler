### 1. Fermi paper — Section 5, *Detectability: D as the Second Fermi Parameter*

**Autonomous AI-Cosmoindustry and the Quiet Expansion Filter: A Threshold-Based
Resolution of the Fermi Paradox** — Ivliev, S. (2026), arXiv:2606.13914
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13914) · verified via arXiv API, published 2026-06-11

**This is prior art on essentially your hypothesis, posted five weeks ago.** Low-mass
probes, robotic seed factories, archival payloads, slow replication, machine-mediated
low-noise expansion, ~10^7 yr to saturate a reachable neighbourhood. A reviewer will
ask why it isn't cited.

Bears on: "Ćirković (2009) makes essentially this point: a model with slow von Neumann
probes naturally produces low detectability and a quiet Galaxy without requiring that
the probes be absent."

Proposed insertion:

> Ivliev (2026) reaches the same structural conclusion from an independent starting
> point: once a civilization attains autonomous AI-driven cosmoindustry, expansion
> proceeds through low-mass probes, robotic seed factories, and archival payloads, so
> any successful expansion is machine-mediated, distributed, and low-noise rather than
> Kardashev-like. The convergence matters, but so does the divergence. Ivliev treats
> post-threshold expansion as too useful and too cheap for any civilization to refuse
> and therefore locates the filter spatially — post-threshold civilizations did not
> arise within reach of the Solar System — whereas the framework here holds G as a free
> parameter and allows a fully capable lineage to be governed into restraint.

Reference: Ivliev, S. (2026). *Autonomous AI-cosmoindustry and the quiet expansion
filter: A threshold-based resolution of the Fermi paradox.* arXiv:2606.13914.

Confidence: high.

**Secondary:** Ivliev's "too rational for all civilizations to refuse" directly
challenges your Section 9 **Interest** and **Governance** filters. Worth a sentence
there too, if you want it.

### 2. Fermi paper — Section 2, *The Classical Self-Replicating Probe Argument*

**The Cosmological Hart-Tipler Conjecture** — Kipping, D. (2026), arXiv:2606.04044
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.04044) · verified via arXiv API, published 2026-06-02,
submitted to *Astrobiology*

Section 2 is built on Hart–Tipler; this is a 2026 minimal-parameter extension of that
same calculation to cosmological volumes.

Bears on: "If technological civilizations have emerged many times, and if even one of
them chose to build reliable self-replicating probes, the Galaxy should not be pristine."

Proposed insertion:

> Kipping (2026) shows the severity does not stop at the Galaxy's edge. Stripping the
> argument back to three parameters — a spontaneous spawn rate, a propagation speed, and
> a start time — he finds that propagation at 0.1c beginning 4.5 Gyr after the Big Bang
> fills over 99.9% of cosmological volume if as few as one galaxy in 10^5 has ever
> spawned a self-replicating lineage. The pristine-Galaxy premise therefore constrains
> not only our own disk but the emergence rate across cosmological volumes, which is why
> the escapes catalogued below must do real work rather than merely be available.

Reference: Kipping, D. (2026). *The cosmological Hart–Tipler conjecture.*
arXiv:2606.04044.

Confidence: high.

**Note:** Kipping models u = 0.1c and near-c only. The abstract gives no slow-u result,
so the insertion deliberately claims none — your 450 km/s is ~0.0015c.

**Metadata note:** the search record carried no arXiv ID or DOI for this paper. The
screening agent flagged it rather than guessing; the ID was recovered by title lookup
against the arXiv API and confirmed (David Kipping, 2026-06-02).

### 3. Fermi paper — Section 5, *Detectability: D as the Second Fermi Parameter*

**Solar System Technosignatures** — Lazio, T. J. W. (2026), arXiv:2606.13797
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.13797) · verified via arXiv API, published 2026-06-11

Section 5 currently asserts the local-non-detection point bare. This is the empirical
anchor for it, and it also supports Section 10's sixth point.

Bears on: "Even within the Solar System, small cold artificial objects in the outer
system would be difficult to distinguish from natural bodies unless they maneuvered,
transmitted, reflected unusually, or emitted waste heat above background."

Proposed insertion:

> This is not a hypothetical concession. Lazio (2026) reviews the actual limits on Solar
> System technosignatures — objects on various orbits and surface artifacts — from
> planetary exploration and astronomical sky surveys, and finds that search completeness
> degrades with distance from the Sun, that only extremely crude upper limits can
> currently be placed, and that in some cases relatively large probes or surface
> artifacts would have escaped detection. The quiet-archive hypothesis therefore does not
> need exotic concealment to survive local non-detection; the outer Solar System has
> simply not been searched well enough to exclude it.

Reference: Lazio, T. J. W. (2026). *Solar System technosignatures.* arXiv:2606.13797.

Confidence: high.

  argument yourself.
