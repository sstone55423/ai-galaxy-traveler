### Fermi paper — Section 10, third point (transient narrow-beam signals)

**A Deep SETI Search for Technosignatures in the TRAPPIST-1 System with FAST** —
Song, G.-Y., Tao, Z.-Z., Huang, B.-L., Yu, B., & Zhang, T.-J. (2026), *The
Astronomical Journal* 172(3), 181
[doi](https://doi.org/10.3847/1538-3881/ae8516) · verified via OpenAlex and
Crossref (title, authors, year, venue, volume/issue/article all match)

Bears on: the existing claim that "non-detection of broad beacons is weak
evidence against such systems," which until now rested only on an
instrument-design argument (Myburgh et al. 2026) with no sensitivity floor
attached. The paper's only other quantitative non-detection anchor is the
mid-IR Dyson-swarm limit.

> The sensitivity floor makes the same point quantitatively. A deep FAST
> pointing at TRAPPIST-1 — five L-band pointings, 1.67 hr on source, ~7.5 Hz
> resolution — reaches a minimum detectable EIRP of about 1.44 × 10^10 W for
> minimally drifting narrowband signals, degrading to about 3.84 × 10^11 W at
> drift rates of 20 Hz/s, and found nothing (Song et al. 2026). A settled node
> whose entire manufacturing load is ~4 kW does not approach that floor except
> by deliberately beaming at Earth with extreme antenna gain during the hours
> the dish happens to be pointed at it; non-detection at 10^10 W is a statement
> about beacons, not about a lineage that never intended to be heard.

Reference added (between Sagan and Tipler): Song, G.-Y., Tao, Z.-Z., Huang,
B.-L., Yu, B., & Zhang, T.-J. (2026). A deep SETI search for technosignatures in
the TRAPPIST-1 system with FAST. *The Astronomical Journal*, 172(3), 181.
