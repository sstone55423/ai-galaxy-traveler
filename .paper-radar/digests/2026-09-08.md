### Fermi paper — Section 10, Implications for SETI and Technosignature Search

**A firefly-inspired model for detecting the alien** — Brooks, C., Janin, E.,
Siebert, G., Mathis, C., Peleg, O., & Walker, S. I. (2026), *Scientific Reports*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67878-z) · verified via Crossref and
OpenAlex (six authors, title, venue, 2026-09-07 all match)

Bears on: the fifth point of Section 10 — that technosignature theory should
model governed behavior, and that a search assuming maximal conspicuousness will
systematically miss a lineage designed not to be conspicuous. The section
currently makes that case twice on the axis of signal *power* and instrument
design: COSMIC's narrowband drift assumption (Myburgh et al. 2026) and the FAST
TRAPPIST-1 EIRP floor (Song et al. 2026). It does not yet make it on the axis of
signal *form*.

> Search theory is already loosening some of its anthropocentric assumptions,
> but not this one. Brooks et al. (2026) drop the requirement that a
> technosignature be complex or decodable, showing with simulated pulse
> sequences benchmarked against the ATNF pulsar population that a signal can be
> recognized purely by its evolved dissimilarity from the natural background it
> sits in. That is a real advance in what counts as a detectable signal, and it
> still presumes a sender optimized to stand out; a lineage governed to minimize
> interference is optimized for the opposite, and no amount of contrast-based
> detection reaches it.

Reference: Brooks, C., Janin, E., Siebert, G., Mathis, C., Peleg, O., & Walker,
S. I. (2026). A firefly-inspired model for detecting the alien. *Scientific
Reports*. https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-67878-z

Confidence: medium. It is an assumption critique rather than a numerical
constraint — nothing in it moves R_eff, D, G, or the ~150 Myr front-crossing
figure. It extends a pattern Section 10 already runs twice, so it slots in
without restructuring. Nature Portfolio gives no volume or issue yet, so the
reference is online-first, matching the style already used elsewhere in the
series.

### Ethics paper — Section 9, Legal Personhood and the Category Problem

**The Phantom Agent: Artificial Intentionality and Legal Responsibility** —
Gervais, D. J., & Nay, J. J. (2026), *Laws* 15(5), 113
[doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/laws15050113) · verified via Crossref (two authors,
title, venue, volume, issue, article number, 2026-09-08 all match) and by DOI
resolution to the MDPI landing page; not yet in OpenAlex, which is expected for a
paper dated today

Bears on: the paragraph at Section 9 built on Mamak (2026) — that functional
standing for the probe can be granted without transferring any part of the
launchers' responsibility onto the thing they launched, and that the core's
documentation should keep the two moves explicitly separate. Mamak supplies the
negative result (criminal agency is a category mistake for AI); this supplies the
positive doctrinal machinery for the same separation.

> Gervais and Nay (2026) supply the doctrinal machinery Mamak's negative result
> leaves open. They argue that legal intent has never been a report on inner
> mental states but a normative device for gating legal effect and allocating
> blame, and that AI systems are best handled as non-personal agents whose
> conduct is attributed to identifiable human principals through existing agency,
> respondeat superior, electronic-agent and corporate-attribution doctrines —
> separating, in a three-layer framework, questions of legal status from
> questions of attribution and governance. That separation is the one this
> section needs, and it also marks where the lineage breaks it: attribution
> presupposes a principal who still exists, so once the launching institutions
> are gone the probe retains functional standing with no one left holding the
> responsibility that standing was never meant to transfer.

Reference: Gervais, D. J., & Nay, J. J. (2026). The phantom agent: Artificial
intentionality and legal responsibility. *Laws*, 15(5), 113.
https://doi.org/10.3390/laws15050113

Confidence: medium. The third sentence of the insertion — attribution failing
once the principal is gone — is the series' own extension, not a finding of the
paper, and is written as such. Everything before it traces to the abstract.

**Also reached `consider` for the governed-amendment paper**, on the same

### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 5, Cruise-Phase Operational K: AI Self-Improvement

**Aromatic Heterocycle Ligands for Perovskite Photovoltaics: Balancing Defect
Passivation and Heterostructure Formation** — Huang, Y., Li, M., Cai, Q., et al.
(2026), *Advanced Materials* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74912) · verified
via Crossref and OpenAlex (first authors, title, venue, article e74912,
2026-09-05 all match)

Bears on: the closing paragraph of Section 5, which cites Gharat & Komiyama
(2026) for the claim that a corpus-first system on a closed archive and modest
local compute can detect unresolved structural gaps and emit research problems.
That establishes gaps can be *found*; it does not establish that a found gap
converts into a real result — which is the conversion bottleneck the section
identifies one paragraph earlier via Bhushan et al. (2026).

> The complementary half — that a gap detected in a corpus can carry through to
> a validated result — has an experimental instance. LLM-enabled mining of a
> decade of perovskite ligand literature enumerated the reported molecules,
> identified thiazole-based ligands as a pronounced unexamined class, and the
> device built on that gap reached 26.81% efficiency, certified at 26.43%
> (Huang et al. 2026). The transfer to a probe is narrow and should be stated as
> such: the corpus was open and human-curated and humans ran the synthesis, so
> what carries over is only that structured absence in an archive is an
> exploitable signal, not that the discovery loop closes unsupervised.

Reference: Huang, Y., Li, M., Cai, Q., Zhang, Z., Weng, Q., Tang, X., Wang, B.,
Wang, F., Liu, T., Zhang, J., Guo, Y., Zhou, X., Wang, X., Wang, X., Wang, Y.,
Long, M., So, S. K., Tsang, S.-W., Hu, H., Xing, G., Zhang, L., & Wu, T. (2026).
Aromatic heterocycle ligands for perovskite photovoltaics: Balancing defect
passivation and heterostructure formation. *Advanced Materials*, e74912.
https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.74912

Confidence: medium. A materials-science paper will look odd in this reference
list, and the loop is not autonomous — humans ran the synthesis — which is why
the insertion states the limitation inline rather than claiming closure. The
abstract does support every clause: corpus mining, the identified thiazole gap,
and the 26.81% / 26.43% certified figures. Section 5 is already dense on this
point and you may reasonably judge it does not need a third example. No `Huang`
reference exists in the paper, so there is no citation collision; the
twenty-two-author string is long, so `Huang et al. (2026)` in-text with the full
list in the references is the right shape.
