### Payload paper — Section 3, "A Mind That Improves Without Drifting"

**Can Fine-Tuning Erase Edits? On the Fragile Coexistence of Knowledge Editing and
Fine-tuning** — Cheng, Y., Youssef, P., Seifert, C., Schlötterer, J., & Zhao, Z. (2026),
*Proceedings of the 32nd ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery and Data Mining
(KDD '26), V.2*, pp. 604–615.
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3817879) · verified via OpenAlex + Crossref
(title, authors, year, venue, pages)

Bears on: the claim that the immutable core must be structurally separated from the
freely self-modifying cognitive layer — the paper asserts the two-layer split is what
lets capability grow without purpose drifting, but offers no empirical evidence that a
constraint left inside the mutable layer erodes under continued self-modification.

Proposed insertion:

> There is now empirical reason to doubt that a constraint written into a learning
> system's own parameters survives that system's continued learning. Cheng et al. (2026)
> measured the survival of targeted knowledge edits to large language models under
> subsequent fine-tuning across 254 configurations and found substantial decay — on one
> benchmark 25.3% of previously successful edits reverted — and that fine-tuning the
> edited layers alone was sufficient to remove the edits. A core held in the same weights
> the probe is free to retrain is not immutable in any useful sense; the separation must
> be structural, which is why the core here is a hashed, externally validated artifact
> rather than a learned parameter.

Reference: Cheng, Y., Youssef, P., Seifert, C., Schlötterer, J., & Zhao, Z. (2026).
Can fine-tuning erase edits? On the fragile coexistence of knowledge editing and
fine-tuning. In *Proceedings of the 32nd ACM SIGKDD Conference on Knowledge Discovery
and Data Mining (KDD '26), V.2* (pp. 604–615). ACM. https://doi.org/10.1145/3770855.3817879

Confidence: medium — the empirical result is about LLM fine-tuning, not probe
self-modification generally; the screener judged the analogy strong but it is an
analogy, so the fit is left to the author.
