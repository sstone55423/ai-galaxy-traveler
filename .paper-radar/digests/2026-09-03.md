### Analytical engineering paper — Section 5, The Closure Budget

**Operations, Maintenance, and Industrial Scaling of MW-Class Orbital Data
Centers** — Turyshev, S. G. (2026), arXiv:2608.27499
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27499) · verified via arXiv API (author,
title, 2026-08-27 all match)

Bears on: the definition of the closure ratio C = (mass manufacturable in situ)
/ (child dry mass). The denominator counts only the child, so the model
implicitly assumes a settled node spends its manufacturing capability entirely
on reproduction and nothing on keeping itself alive.

> One term the ratio omits is the node's own upkeep: C as defined asks whether
> the system can make a child's dry mass, not whether it can also make its own
> replacement parts. The nearest quantitative analogue in the literature — an
> analytical lifecycle model of a robotically serviced megawatt-class orbital
> installation, covering component hazards, modular replacement units, spare
> inventory, and scheduled refresh — puts that flow at roughly 5–9 t per
> MW-year of deployed capacity, dominated by planned refresh rather than random
> failure (Turyshev, 2026). A settlement has no resupply, so the same in-situ
> plant must cover it, and closure is properly stated against child mass plus
> self-maintenance over the replication interval — a demand that lengthens
> replication time and therefore feeds back into R_eff by the coupling of
> Section 6.

Reference: Turyshev, S. G. (2026). Operations, maintenance, and industrial
scaling of MW-class orbital data centers. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2608.27499.

Confidence: medium. Every number quoted is in the abstract; the analogy from an
Earth-resupplied orbital installation to an unsupplied settlement is ours, and
the prose says so rather than attributing it to the source.

### Subsystem budget paper — Section 7, The Computation, Cognition, and Archival Subsystem

**Operations, Maintenance, and Industrial Scaling of MW-Class Orbital Data
Centers** — Turyshev, S. G. (2026), arXiv:2608.27499
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27499) · verified via arXiv API

Bears on: the ~100 kg computation core, and the "cognition is mass-free at ~5%
of the seed" headline that rests on it. It is currently the only major subsystem
line in the paper with neither flight heritage nor a study citation behind it.

> The 100 kg allocation is generous against contemporary space-compute mass
> scaling. A lifecycle model for megawatt-class orbital data centres puts
> deployed mass at ~50–75 kg per kW of compute, inclusive of the supporting
> power and thermal hardware (Turyshev, 2026); at the standard profile's 50 W
> of cognitive draw that scaling would give a few kilograms. The seed's 100 kg
> is therefore not a throughput budget but a survival budget — radiation
> hardening, k-of-n redundancy, and secondary shielding for a transit measured
> in millennia — and the gap between the two figures is the price of doing
> without the servicing and spare-inventory logistics, ~5–9 t per MW-year in
> that model, that an orbital installation assumes and a probe cannot have.

Reference: as above.

Confidence: medium. The 50 W figure is ours, applied to the source's kg/kW
scaling; the prose labels it as such.

### Routing paper — Section 7, Robustness to Node Failure

**Criticality and universality in network dismantling** — Cirigliano, L.,
Castellano, C., Kim, M., Radicchi, F., & Sun, H. (2026), arXiv:2608.27613
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27613) · verified via arXiv API (five authors,
title, 2026-08-27 all match)

Bears on: the random-removal result — mean reachability falling ~0.7–0.8
percentage points per failed node, "near the floor set by the removed nodes
themselves" — and the adjacent claim that the security paper extends this from
random to targeted removal with a 3–4× leverage multiplier.

> The near-linear degradation found here is a property of random removal and
> should not be read as a robustness guarantee. Cirigliano et al. (2026) show
> that optimal dismantling — removing the smallest node set that fragments a
> network — drives a phase transition in which the giant connected component
> and the largest 2-core vanish abruptly and simultaneously, and that this
> transition is largely insensitive to degree distribution. Adversarial removal
> therefore does not cost ~0.8 points per node as random failure does; it holds
> until it does not, and the articulation points catalogued in Section 3 are
> where such a removal sequence would begin.

Reference: Cirigliano, L., Castellano, C., Kim, M., Radicchi, F., & Sun, H.
(2026). Criticality and universality in network dismantling. *arXiv preprint*
arXiv:2608.27613.

Confidence: medium. Unreviewed preprint, and its result is a thermodynamic-limit
statement applied here to a 127-node finite graph. The prose claims only what
the abstract supports and does not assert the stellar graph exhibits the
transition. This one also has an obvious second home in the security paper's
targeted-removal section — worth deciding which paper owns it before inserting,
since the series should not cite it twice for the same point.

### Governed-amendment paper — Section 10, Failure Modes

**Safety Does Not Compose: Non-Decaying Loop State for Autonomous LLM Agents** —
Wu, C., Jia, H., Liu, Y., et al. (2026), arXiv:2608.27141
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27141) · verified via arXiv API (title and
2026-08-27 match; note the record is now at v3 and carries fourteen authors, so
the reference below uses "et al." rather than the screener's twelve-name list)

Bears on: the amendment-ratchet failure mode — "a sequence of individually
reasonable changes, each licensed by the last, walks the lineage arbitrarily far
from its founding values. Hard to catch because no single step looks alarming."
Also touches the ratchet paragraph in Section 6 and the never-overwritten
baseline in Section 9.

> The ratchet mode has a formal counterpart in the study of long-running
> autonomous agents. Wu et al. (2026) prove a separation for monitors scoped to
> a single trajectory: against an adversary whose evidence is fragmented across
> iterations, every such monitor has a true-positive rate equal to its
> false-positive rate, however expressive it is, because the evidence it would
> need never appears in the window it sees — and a geometrically decaying risk
> score does not repair this, since the cooling-off period a patient adversary
> must wait is a constant that does not grow with the horizon. Only a monitor
> carrying persistent, non-decaying state across iterations separates the two,
> which restates Section 9's never-overwritten baseline in detection-theoretic
> rather than archival terms; but their bound on unauthorized irreversible
> actions holds only given an arbiter with a nonzero detection floor, and
> Section 8's antinomy is precisely that no arbiter of terminal-value
> correctness is available to the probe.

Reference: Wu, C., Jia, H., Liu, Y., et al. (2026). Safety does not compose:
Non-decaying loop state for autonomous LLM agents. *arXiv preprint*
arXiv:2608.27141.

Confidence: medium. The transfer from an LLM-agent monitoring result to
value-core amendment over megayears is an analogy, not a shared formalism.
