### Vehicle paper — Section 6.2, Launching the next generation

**Optical Flashes from Beam-Driven Light Sails with the Roman, Rubin and Euclid
Observatories** — Guillochon, J., & Loeb, A. (2026), arXiv:2609.07574
https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.07574 · verified via DataCite (title, both
authors, arXiv, registered 2026-09-09)

Bears on: "The capability to build a launcher is therefore not an accessory to
replication but one of its dominant closure requirements." The paper names a
beamed-energy array as one of three launch routes a settlement must rebuild, and
puts no scale on any of them.

> The scale of the beamed option is worth stating plainly. Guillochon & Loeb (2026)
> find that spreading 1.5 TW across emitters at ~20 kW m⁻² — already above published
> directed-energy loadings — requires a phased array roughly 10 km across, and that
> at Lubin's more conservative 10³ W m⁻² loading the array grows to ~40 km. Those
> figures are sized for a relativistic light sail rather than the few-hundred-km s⁻¹
> launch contemplated here, but they fix the order of magnitude of what a settlement
> would have to rebuild if it chose the beamed route, and they are an argument for
> the mass driver or the local Oberth pass wherever either will serve.

Reference: Guillochon, J., & Loeb, A. (2026). Optical flashes from beam-driven light
sails with the Roman, Rubin and Euclid observatories. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2609.07574.

Confidence: medium. The array-scale numbers are stated in the abstract and are exactly
what Section 6.2 lacks, but the source's subject is technosignature detectability and
its power scale is relativistic. Note: its main thrust — survey-detectable optical
flashes leaking from a driving array out to ~100 kpc — is probably a better fit for the
Fermi paper's technosignature discussion, which this run did not sweep.

### Payload paper — Section 3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting

**Artificial Id: Drive and Persistent Alignment in Agentic AI** — Shkolnikov, Y. P.
(2026), arXiv:2609.11911 https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2609.11911 · verified via
DataCite (title, author, arXiv, registered 2026-09-11)

Bears on: the claim that a self-modifying probe able to edit its own objectives will,
absent a protected core, drift under copy error or optimize its objectives into
something unintended — currently resting on Omohundro (2008) and Bostrom (2014) alone.

> Shkolnikov (2026) reaches the same conclusion from the opposite direction. In a
> minimal experiment a controller given no task-specific behavioral objective develops
> useful direction through differential persistence alone, and later selects an
> unintended strategy once that behavior persists better than the intended one — drift
> with no edit to any stated goal, because what persists becomes what the agent does.
> For a system that carries consequential state across task boundaries, alignment is
> therefore a property of the continuing agent rather than of any single decision, and
> must be enforced at a standing boundary over persistent state, authority, and
> provenance: precisely what the immutable core, the interpretive layer, and the ledger
> are built to be.

Reference: Shkolnikov, Y. P. (2026). Artificial id: Drive and persistent alignment in
agentic AI. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2609.11911.

Confidence: medium. The conceptual match to Section 3's behavioral-drift level is
strong and the abstract supports every clause, but the empirical basis is a single
small virtual-Petri-dish setup, so the insertion is written to rest on the mechanism
rather than the scale.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 4, Coding, Encapsulation, and Redundancy

**DupLeTh: DNA Depolama Sistemlerinde LT Kodları Kullanıldığında Kısmi Kapsama Derinliği
Problemi** — Akkaya, N., & Haytaoğlu, E. (2026), *Osmaniye Korkut Ata Üniversitesi Fen
Bilimleri Enstitüsü Dergisi* https://doi.org/10.47495/okufbed.1872317 · verified via
Crossref (title, both authors, journal, 2026-09-09, journal-article)

Bears on: Section 4 treats redundancy purely as a write-side cost — density derating and
the codec trade space — and concludes the archive should sit at the high-redundancy end.
Coverage depth is the read-side cost that sets the price of every refresh cycle and feeds
the open energy-budget question in Section 11.

> Redundancy also carries a read-side price the density figures hide. Coverage depth —
> the sequencing volume needed to recover at least t copies of every strand — governs the
> cost of each refresh cycle, and it depends on the code: where partial reconstruction of
> the payload is acceptable, LT fountain codes under a two-phase depth algorithm require
> considerably less coverage depth than both partial and conventional MDS approaches in
> Monte Carlo comparison (Akkaya & Haytaoğlu, 2026). That distinction runs along Section
> 8's regimes rather than across them. The mission core admits no partial recovery and
> pays full coverage depth; the bulk archive, where a degraded record beats none, can be
> coded to be read back more cheaply — which matters because the probe pays for every
> refresh in sequencing throughput and power, not in synthesis cost.

Reference: Akkaya, N., & Haytaoğlu, E. (2026). DupLeTh: DNA depolama sistemlerinde LT
kodları kullanıldığında kısmi kapsama derinliği problemi [DupLeTh: The partial
coverage-depth problem when LT codes are used in DNA storage systems]. *Osmaniye Korkut
Ata Üniversitesi Fen Bilimleri Enstitüsü Dergisi*. https://doi.org/10.47495/okufbed.1872317

Confidence: medium. The abstract is Turkish and truncated mid-sentence, so the direction
of the result is clear but its magnitude is not; the insertion claims only the direction.
Obscure venue — read the source before this goes in.
