### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 4, Coding, Encapsulation, and Redundancy

**Methodological advances and computational frameworks in ancient DNA research:
a narrative review** — Luvanga, J., Akwo, A., Ketu, K., Khamala, A. A., &
Onsoti, S. O. (2026), *Egyptian Journal of Forensic Sciences*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1186/s41935-026-00576-z) · verified via OpenAlex

Bears on: the layered-defence claim that Reed–Solomon and fountain codes recover
the logical payload from physically damaged strands, and upstream of it Section
3's renewability argument that the probe periodically reads, validates, repairs
and re-synthesises the archive. The read side of that cycle — extracting usable
sequence from fragmented, chemically damaged templates, and telling authentic
damage from corruption — is currently unsourced.

> The read side of the refresh cycle has an existing discipline behind it.
> Ancient-DNA work routinely recovers sequence from heavily fragmented,
> chemically damaged templates, using silica-based and single-stranded
> extraction tuned for ultrashort fragments, damage-aware library preparation,
> and bioinformatic pipelines built for short, error-prone reads (Luvanga et
> al., 2026). That literature also supplies the authentication criteria a
> self-renewing archive needs before it rewrites anything — fragment-length
> distribution, nucleotide misincorporation patterns, and contamination
> controls — which are what distinguish an authentically decayed strand from a
> corrupted or foreign one, and it confirms that the governing preservation
> variables are environmental rather than intrinsic to the molecule.

Reference: Luvanga, J., Akwo, A., Ketu, K., Khamala, A. A., & Onsoti, S. O.
(2026). Methodological advances and computational frameworks in ancient DNA
research: A narrative review. *Egyptian Journal of Forensic Sciences*.
https://doi.org/10.1186/s41935-026-00576-z

Confidence: medium. It is a narrative review in a forensic-sciences venue, not a
primary result. It gives no longevity figure, so it must not be used to
reinforce or revise the Allentoft 521-yr half-life or the Grass megayear
extrapolation.

### Ethics paper — Section 9, Legal Personhood and the Category Problem

**AI agency and criminal responsibility: a category mistake** — Mamak, K.
(2026), *Inquiry* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174x.2026.2720904) ·
verified via OpenAlex

Bears on: the Section 9 argument that the probe should get functional standing
decoupled from sentience. The section does not address the mirror-image move —
whether granting standing also relocates responsibility onto the probe and away
from the launchers — which matters because Section 1 frames the launch as the
one-way door where responsibility attaches.

> Standing runs in one direction only. Mamak (2026) argues that applying the
> framework of criminal agency to current and foreseeable AI systems is a
> category mistake — no existing system satisfies the conditions for being an
> agent that can be punished or held responsible — and that abandoning the
> agentic framing redirects attention to the human duties, decisions, and
> failures that precede the harm. His scope does not reach a
> ten-thousandth-generation probe, but it constrains the near end of the lineage
> exactly where this paper needs it: the launch decision. Functional standing
> for the probe can therefore be granted without transferring any part of the
> launchers' responsibility onto the thing they launched, and the core's
> documentation should keep the two moves explicitly separate, because
> conflating them is the most convenient error available to anyone who wants the
> lineage built.

Reference: Mamak, K. (2026). AI agency and criminal responsibility: A category
mistake. *Inquiry*. Advance online publication.
https://doi.org/10.1080/0020174x.2026.2720904

Confidence: medium.

### Ethics paper — Section 7, Whose Values? The Content of the Immutable Core

**The embodied ethics alignment problem of AI** — Zwitter, A. (2026), *Discover
Artificial Intelligence* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01905-w) ·
verified via OpenAlex

Bears on: the minimalism-versus-proceduralism choice. Both options assume the
core's content is the kind of thing that can be fixed as encoded content at all;
Zwitter challenges that premise, and the challenge favours the proceduralist
half of the paper's own answer.

> A prior objection cuts beneath the choice between minimalism and
> proceduralism. Zwitter (2026) argues that ethics is not primarily a
> formalizable rule-set, preference ordering, or optimization target but an
> emergent property of embodiment, affect, vulnerability, social embeddedness,
> teleological orientation, and the developmental formation of character — so a
> system lacking those conditions may be behaviorally steered toward acceptable
> outcomes without instantiating moral agency in the strong sense the alignment
> literature usually implies. If that is right, an immutable core of fixed
> content is a steering mechanism and not a conscience, and the honest
> description of it is the weaker one. It is also an argument for proceduralism
> over minimalism: a standing obligation to keep re-examining ethical claims is
> the closest a fixed artifact gets to the developmental formation Zwitter says
> substantive moral content presupposes.

Reference: Zwitter, A. (2026). The embodied ethics alignment problem of AI.
*Discover Artificial Intelligence*.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s44163-026-01905-w

Confidence: medium.
