### Knowledge-growth paper — "Cruise as an active knowledge-growth phase" / the growth term G(t)

**Auto Research for Materials: Auditable AI-Scientist Workflows with Held-Out
Transfer** — Ning, J., Li, X., Zhong, S., Zeng, J., & Ke, G. (2026), arXiv:2607.17100
[abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.17100) · verified via arXiv API (title, author
list, and 2026-07-19 date confirmed)

Bears on: the claim that the probe grows knowledge autonomously during cruise
(G(t) > 0). This paper supplies a discipline on that term — an autonomous science
loop that selects among its own hypotheses on its own validation data can inflate
apparent gains that do not survive untouched evidence, so a result should count
toward G(t) only after held-out verification.

Proposed insertion (author's call — held back from staging because confidence is
medium and the source is a fresh, not-yet-indexed preprint):

> Not every apparent gain is a real one: an autonomous science loop that selects
> among its own hypotheses on its own validation data can overfit that loop and
> book improvements that vanish against untouched evidence. In a controlled test
> across ten materials-prediction endpoints, 9 of 10 model changes chosen in the
> loop still held up when frozen and scored once on a never-seen holdout (Ning et
> al., 2026). This sets a discipline on the growth term: a candidate result
> contributes to G(t) only after it survives held-out verification against
> reserved observations, so that K accumulates knowledge that generalizes rather
> than artifacts of the probe's own validation.
