### Knowledge growth -- Generation, the G(t) term

**Reinforcement learning guided robotic platform that investigates and discovers
asymmetric reactions** -- Klochowicz, P., Kassir, A. F., Parida, C., & Granda,
J. M. (2026), *Nature Communications*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78271-9) - verified via Crossref,
peer-reviewed.

Bears on: that autonomous knowledge generation without human supervision is a
demonstrated capability rather than a projection, and therefore that G(t) can be
treated as nonzero for a probe operating alone.

> Autonomous discovery is not a projection. A reinforcement-learning-guided
> robotic platform working in asymmetric catalysis made more than 3,000
> experimental decisions over 30 days and returned four new highly
> enantioselective transformations, with its control policies trained on a
> digital twin of the chemistry before transfer to physical hardware
> (Klochowicz et al., 2026). The chemistry is beside the point; the ratio is
> not -- roughly 750 committed decisions per genuinely new result in a narrow,
> well-instrumented space, and a simulated rehearsal before any irreversible
> commitment of material. A probe works under the same rule, rehearsing in its
> own model and spending mass only on the experiment the model cannot settle.

Reference: Klochowicz, P., Kassir, A. F., Parida, C., & Granda, J. M. (2026).
Reinforcement learning guided robotic platform that investigates and discovers
asymmetric reactions. *Nature Communications*.
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-78271-9

Confidence: medium. The strongest item of the day -- peer-reviewed and squarely
on the yardstick.

### Knowledge growth -- Observational and operational knowledge

**Autonomous Science Bench: Evaluating AI Agents for Autonomous Scientific
Discovery** -- Wen, Y., & Liu, H. (2026), arXiv:2610.18449
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18449) - verified via arXiv API, posted
2026-10-13.

Bears on: that K decomposes into observational and operational knowledge, and
that the two components do not grow in lockstep.

> The split between observational and operational knowledge is not bookkeeping;
> the two come apart under measurement. Autonomous Science Bench scores budgeted
> design-build-test-learn campaigns through two separate gates -- a discovery
> gate on the designs the agent actually measured, and a learning gate on how
> well its model ranks designs it never tried -- and finds agents that clear one
> and fail the other: some build accurate rankings while finding too few
> promising designs, others find promising designs but cannot predict which
> unseen ones will work (Wen and Liu, 2026). The best configuration succeeded in
> 67% of 144 trials and the hardest campaign in only 3 of 24, so G(t) should be
> budgeted against a per-campaign success rate well below unity, and a probe
> that has observed a great deal is not thereby a probe that has learned a great
> deal.

Reference: Wen, Y., & Liu, H. (2026). Autonomous Science Bench: Evaluating AI
Agents for Autonomous Scientific Discovery. *arXiv*:2610.18449.
https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.18449

Confidence: medium. Preprint, v0.1 benchmark release.

### Payload -- The immutable value core, value stability under self-modification

**Verifier-Grounded Agentic RL for Provably Correct Code** -- Ma, G., Zhang, Y.,
He, B., Shui, Z., Erata, F., Ramanathan, M. K., Sojoudi, S., & Zhou, H. (2026),
arXiv:2610.19298 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.19298) - verified via arXiv
API, posted 2026-10-13.

Bears on: that the value core can remain immutable while the cognitive layer
modifies itself freely -- that a self-modifying system can be prevented from
rewriting or reinterpreting the specification it is judged against.

> A self-modifying system can always satisfy its own checks by weakening them,
> and the remedy is structural rather than statistical. Ma et al. (2026) train
> code agents against a live program verifier and find the reward must be
> subordinate to an integrity audit that outranks the verifier, rejecting any
> candidate that alters the specification or assumes what it was meant to prove;
> scoring by the weighted fraction of proof obligations discharged, extended
> with hidden probes from a reference proof, raises held-out verification from
> 66.1% to 93.5% on DafnyBench and from 56.3% to 100% on SWE-Proof, beating a
> binary pass-or-fail reward trained identically. The probe's value core sits on
> the audit side of that line: the cognitive layer may propose any change to
> itself, but nothing it proposes may reach the text it is checked against.

Reference: Ma, G., Zhang, Y., He, B., Shui, Z., Erata, F., Ramanathan, M. K.,
Sojoudi, S., & Zhou, H. (2026). Verifier-Grounded Agentic RL for Provably
Correct Code. *arXiv*:2610.19298. https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.19298

Confidence: medium. The transfer from program verification to a value core is an
analogy, not a result about values.

### Payload -- The interpretive layer

**Computational Reflective Equilibrium for Agentic AI: A Rawlsian Alignment
Scaffold for Governance** -- Zhu, Q. (2026), arXiv:2610.16015
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16015) - verified via arXiv API, posted
2026-10-12.

Bears on: that an interpretive layer can apply a fixed value core to
unanticipated situations across millennia without revising the core, and that
such interpretation settles rather than drifting.

> Zhu (2026) gives this mediation a formal shape: agentic reflective equilibrium
> links principles, considered judgments, tool evidence and candidate actions
> through typed support and attack relations, with provenance encoding the
> instruction hierarchy, and the agent acts on an equilibrium of that state. The
> framework establishes lexical priority of higher-authority sources, stability
> certificates, and a quarantine theorem against injected instructions -- the
> properties an interpretive layer must have if it is to reason about novel
> cases for millennia without ever amending the principles it reasons from. That
> its equilibria are Nash equilibria of a mutual-adjustment game is a useful
> handle on why interpretation converges instead of wandering.

Reference: Zhu, Q. (2026). Computational Reflective Equilibrium for Agentic AI:
A Rawlsian Alignment Scaffold for Governance. *arXiv*:2610.16015.
https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.16015

Confidence: medium. Single-author preprint; the Rawlsian framing connects to the
ethics paper's existing use of Rawls.

### DNA mission-ledger -- archival substrate, the write path

**Archival data storage using DNA proximity graphs** -- Perry, E., Tiepelt, J.,
& Jacobson, J. (2026), *bioRxiv* preprint
[doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758707) - verified via Crossref;
posted-content, container bioRxiv.

Bears on: that writing the ledger to DNA requires de novo oligonucleotide
synthesis, and that synthesis cost sets the floor on how much the probe can
afford to commit to the archive.

> The write path need not be de novo synthesis. Perry, Tiepelt and Jacobson
> (2026) store data not in synthesized sequence but in the spatial adjacency of
> randomly barcoded molecules: barcodes are deposited onto a patterned surface,
> an enzymatic step records neighbouring pairs, and sequencing those pairs
> yields a proximity graph from which the original pattern is reconstructed. The
> demonstration is small -- 136 bits written by photolithography, 114 recovered
> -- but the cost structure is the point: writing is already cheaper than
> synthesis-based schemes and projects roughly five orders of magnitude cheaper
> with established optical writing, at the price of more expensive readout. That
> trade suits the mission ledger exactly, because each entry is written once and
> read almost never.

Reference: Perry, E., Tiepelt, J., & Jacobson, J. (2026). Archival data storage
using DNA proximity graphs. *bioRxiv* preprint.
https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.12.758707

Confidence: medium. Preprint, 136-bit demonstration.

### Fermi -- technosignature non-detection, the D term

**Millimetre/Sub-Millimetre SETI: Expanding the Radio Technosignature Parameter
Space with ALMA2040** -- Mason, L. A., Garrett, M. A., Sheikh, S. Z., Wandia,
K., Richards, A. M. S., Gajjar, V., Tremblay, C. D., & Siemion, A. P. V. (2026),
arXiv:2610.22266 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22266) - verified via arXiv
API, posted 2026-10-15, 8 authors confirmed.

Bears on: that the absence of detected technosignatures places no useful
constraint on a slow, non-signalling, low-power expansion, because existing and
planned searches are tuned to high-power beacons and to civilisation-scale waste
heat.

> The searches themselves make the point. Mason et al. (2026) note that the
> millimetre and sub-millimetre sky is almost entirely unsearched despite being
> the regime a civilisation optimising directed, high-data-rate links would
> plausibly choose, and that the two broadband channels ALMA2040 could open are
> aggregate leakage from Kardashev Type I emitters -- a roughly 60 pc horizon
> for one hour on source -- and Dysonian cold waste heat from large-scale
> computation. Neither threshold has any purchase on the vehicle described here:
> a cruise draw of 50-150 W, with a 4 kW reactor rating reached only during
> manufacturing, sits many orders of magnitude below a Type I budget, and the
> waste heat of a single seed is indistinguishable from cold dust.
> Non-detection is therefore consistent with D held near zero by physics rather
> than by rarity, and the D term in R_eff x D x G should be read as a statement
> about what we can see, not about what is there.

Reference: Mason, L. A., Garrett, M. A., Sheikh, S. Z., Wandia, K., Richards,
A. M. S., Gajjar, V., Tremblay, C. D., & Siemion, A. P. V. (2026).
Millimetre/Sub-Millimetre SETI: Expanding the Radio Technosignature Parameter
Space with ALMA2040. *arXiv*:2610.22266.
https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.22266

Confidence: medium. **Correction applied before recording:** the screening agent
wrote "some twenty orders of magnitude below a Type I budget". That is wrong.
150 W against a Type I budget is roughly ten orders on the ~10^12 W reading and
roughly fourteen on Kardashev's original ~10^16 W reading. The prose above says
"many orders of magnitude"; substitute the exact figure for whichever Type I
definition the paper already uses.

### Governance -- planetary protection and noninterference

**The Obligation to Conduct Environmental Impact Assessments in the Sustainable
Development of Mega-Constellations** -- Zha, H. (2026), *Transnational
Environmental Law* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1017/s2047102526100405) - verified
via Crossref, peer-reviewed.

Bears on: that Outer Space Treaty Article IX's duty to avoid harmful
contamination and adverse changes is the legal root of the probe's
noninterference obligation, and that the probe must discharge it without
supervision.

> Article IX is not a bare prohibition. Recent space-law scholarship reads it as
> carrying a procedural obligation to assess impact before acting, with Article
> III importing general international environmental-assessment law into outer
> space for any activity likely to cause significant adverse effect (Zha, 2026).
> Two features of that reading matter here: it fixes the duty on a responsible
> state, and it turns on a significance threshold applied before the activity. A
> probe arriving at a target centuries after launch has neither a state to
> answer to nor a channel to file with one, so the assessment must be
> internalized -- the E0-E7 ladder is that assessment, run by the probe on
> itself, with the significance threshold encoded as the rung at which
> capability-yes/release-no binds.

Reference: Zha, H. (2026). The Obligation to Conduct Environmental Impact
Assessments in the Sustainable Development of Mega-Constellations.
*Transnational Environmental Law*. https://doi.org/10.1017/s2047102526100405

Confidence: medium.

### Governed amendment -- the legitimacy problem

**Liberal first, democratic second: Moving the EU from militant liberalism to
supranational democratic defense** -- Verovsek, P. J. (2026), *Global
Constitutionalism* [doi](https://doi.org/10.1017/s2045381726100501) - verified
via Crossref, peer-reviewed.

Bears on: that an immutable value core cannot be legitimately amended by the
lineage that inherits it, because the inheritors were not party to its framing.

> Constitutional theory has met this asymmetry before. Verovsek (2026) treats
> the European Union as a supranational militant democracy -- a polity that
> entrenches liberal commitments ahead of democratic ones and enforces them
> against states that acceded long after the framing -- and argues that the
> entrenched core should be procedural rather than substantive, on the grounds
> that constitutional principles survive best where basic disagreement about
> foundations is respected rather than overruled. The lineage problem takes the
> same shape: a core that entrenches an amendment procedure remains contestable
> by descendants, while a core that entrenches a value list can only be obeyed
> or broken, which is why the charter's unamendable clauses are the weakest part
> of the design and not the strongest.

Reference: Verovsek, P. J. (2026). Liberal first, democratic second: Moving the
EU from militant liberalism to supranational democratic defense. *Global
Constitutionalism*. https://doi.org/10.1017/s2045381726100501

Confidence: medium. The single best-matched item for this paper's own
literature.

### Roadmap -- S4, settlement zero and the interstellar launcher

**Wireless Energy Transfer for Space Applications: A Review of Technologies,
Mission Architectures, Energy Routing, and System Integration** -- Lin, L.,
Feng, Z., Miele, E., Ge, Q., Iori, M., Liu, C., Chau, K.-T., Garoli, D., &
Proietti Zaccaria, R. (2026), arXiv:2610.16960
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16960) - verified via arXiv API, posted
2026-10-12, 9 authors confirmed.

Bears on: that the launcher built at settlement zero may be either a mass driver
of order 10^3-10^4 km or a beamed array, and that neither option is presently
validated at the required scale.

> The beamed array is often treated as the easier of the two launcher options,
> but it is not the more validated one. A recent survey of wireless energy
> transfer for space finds maturity sharply uneven: high-power ground
> demonstrations and short-range flight applications are established, while
> long-range radiative transfer between spacecraft and deep-space delivery rest
> on models, simulations and mission studies rather than hardware (Lin et al.,
> 2026). Both launcher paths therefore enter the ladder at the same rung, and
> settlement zero must carry whichever one it chooses through its own
> build-and-fire demonstration before first dispatch.

Reference: Lin, L., Feng, Z., Miele, E., Ge, Q., Iori, M., Liu, C., Chau, K.-T.,
Garoli, D., & Proietti Zaccaria, R. (2026). Wireless energy transfer for space
applications: A review of technologies, mission architectures, energy routing,
and system integration. *arXiv*:2610.16960.
https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2610.16960

Confidence: medium. This one paper surfaced on four separate screens (roadmap,
bootstrapping, subsystem budget, home programme). The launcher-maturity reading
above is the best single home for it; its secondary point, that beaming
efficiencies are incomparable without a declared energy reference plane, is the
same bookkeeping discipline the series already applies in separating reactor
rating from manufacturing load from cruise draw, and could be noted in the
analytical engineering paper instead.

## Why nothing was staged

Step 5 stages only high-confidence cites. All fifteen proposals came back
medium, and the reason is the same in every case: the screening agents are given
each paper's title and thesis string, not its manuscript, so none could confirm
that a candidate bears on a specific existing claim or that the series does not
already cite the work. Several said so explicitly and marked their section names
as inferred. Under that constraint medium is the honest ceiling, and an
auto-staged insertion anchored to a guessed heading is exactly the failure this
pipeline exists to avoid.

The repo was left as found: branch `main`, HEAD 9f378b4, same modified-file
count, no worktree created, no lock files touched.

## Calibration note

Fifteen proposed cites from one 413-candidate sweep is seven to fifteen times
the rate the screening instruction asks for ("a typical day produces zero to two
across the whole series"). Each agent sees only its own paper, so each verdict
of one or two looks locally reasonable while the series total runs hot. Two
options worth considering: tell each agent the series-wide budget and that it is
shared, or add a second pass that ranks all proposed cites against each other
and keeps the top one or two. Today's ten proposals should be read as a shortlist
to triage, not as ten accepted insertions.

## Query tuning observed

- `network`: 11 of 15 candidates were blockchain, smart-agriculture and
  IoT-security collisions. The query appears to match bare "network", "routing"
  and "AI"; it should require DTN, bundle protocol, store-and-forward,
  intermittent connectivity or failure-detection terms.
- `speciation`: 18 of 19 were biological or chemical speciation collisions
  (salmonid introgression, barcoding taxonomy, metal speciation). The paper's
  concepts are divergence thresholds and reversibility, not speciation as such.
- `home_programme`: "intercept" caught three random-intercept cross-lagged panel
  studies; "mission" caught Protestant mission histories and
  mission-oriented innovation policy. Both belong in that paper's `exclude`
  list.
- Abstract coverage was poor across the sweep -- many Crossref records arrived
  with no abstract at all, which forced otherwise-judgeable candidates down to
  flagged. Backfilling abstracts from OpenAlex for Crossref-sourced records
  would raise screening yield more than any query change.
