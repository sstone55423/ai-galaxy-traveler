### Fermi paper — Section 10, Implications for SETI and Technosignature Search

**The VLA and High-Frequency SETI: Expanding the Search for Life** — Myburgh,
T., Stiegler, N., Tremblay, C. D., Bright, J. S., & Donnachie, R. A. (2026),
*Acta Astronautica* (in press), arXiv:2608.18275
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.18275) ·
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2026.08.032) · verified via arXiv API
(authors, title, DOI all match)

Bears on: the section's third SETI implication — that search should attend to
transient narrow-beam signals, because a quiet lineage communicates rarely and
directionally, so non-detection of broad beacons is weak evidence against it.
The citation grounds what current instruments are actually tuned for instead of
leaving "classical SETI" as an unsupported characterisation.

> Current instruments are built for the opposite case. The COSMIC system on the
> VLA searches commensally for narrowband signals drifting in frequency at
> rates up to ±50 Hz/s, beamforming toward Gaia-catalogue stars in the field of
> view (Myburgh et al., 2026). That design assumes a persistent,
> Doppler-drifting carrier — precisely the signature a lineage communicating
> rarely and directionally would not produce.

Reference: Myburgh, T., Stiegler, N., Tremblay, C. D., Bright, J. S., &
Donnachie, R. A. (2026). The VLA and high-frequency SETI: Expanding the search
for life. *Acta Astronautica*. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.actaastro.2026.08.032

Caveats: the abstract reports candidate hits still under imaging follow-up, not
a null result, so the prose is confined to search *design*. **Metadata
discrepancy:** OpenAlex returns `publication_year: 2025` for this DOI while
arXiv posts it 2026-08-18 and the DOI string carries `2026`. Almost certainly an
Elsevier available-online/issue-year artifact, but confirm the year before the
reference is finalised.

### Payload paper — Section 3, A Mind That Improves Without Drifting

**Continual-learning rules shape representational drift** — Si, Y., & Qin, S.
(2026), arXiv:2608.16141 [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16141) · verified
via arXiv API (title and both authors match; posted 2026-08-17)

Bears on: the section's architectural claim that stability of purpose and
unbounded growth of capability must be separated *structurally* — "This
separation is what allows capability to grow without purpose drifting" — rather
than by damping the learner to hold values steady. Place after the Nijjer (2026)
paragraph.

> The same experimental literature warns against the obvious alternative —
> buying stability by anchoring the learner itself. Training convolutional and
> recurrent networks on task sequences under different continual-learning rules,
> Si and Qin (2026) find that mechanisms which strongly anchor weights nearly
> freeze internal representations, and that directly suppressing
> representational drift during replay impairs acquisition of subsequent tasks;
> drift's magnitude is set by whichever mechanism protects old knowledge.
> Stability and plasticity trade off within a single learning system, which is
> why the architecture buys stability structurally — a small core reproduced
> exactly, outside the learner — rather than by constraining the cognitive
> layer's capacity to change.

Reference: Si, Y., & Qin, S. (2026). Continual-learning rules shape
representational drift. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2608.16141.

### Knowledge-growth paper — Section 5, Cruise-Phase Operational K: AI Self-Improvement

**SGHA: Evidence-Grounded Research Problem Discovery with Local Language
Models** — Gharat, S., & Komiyama, J. (2026), arXiv:2608.17501
[arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.17501) · verified via arXiv API (title and
both authors match; posted 2026-08-18)

Bears on: the claim that during cruise the probe works from an active working
memory subset rather than the full DNA archive, and that this bounds what the
cognitive layer can reference. The candidate suggests the bound is weaker than
it looks.

> This bound is less severe than it appears. A corpus-first research-problem
> discovery system running entirely on a locally served 9B open-weight model —
> no external queries, no proprietary API — structures a fixed literature corpus
> into an evidence graph, detects unresolved structural gaps across it, and
> emits research problems with traceable assumptions and success criteria
> (Gharat & Komiyama, 2026). The configuration is the probe's: a closed archive,
> modest local compute, and a requirement that every generated claim carry
> provenance back to the evidence that produced it.

Reference: Gharat, S., & Komiyama, J. (2026). SGHA: Evidence-grounded research
problem discovery with local language models. *arXiv preprint* arXiv:2608.17501.

Caveat: the abstract reports no benchmark quality numbers, so the prose asserts
only the architecture and its demonstration, not performance.
