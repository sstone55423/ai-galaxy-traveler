### Payload paper — Section 6, The Galactic Network

**Mind Viruses: Self-Propagating Ideas in Multi-Agent LLM Systems** — Papadopoulos,
V., Shah, M., Zimmerman, S., & Lindsey, J. (2026), arXiv:2608.10218
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10218) · verified via arXiv API 2026-08-16

Bears on: the "galactic attack surface" passage, which currently locates the
threat in *updates* — signed design changes and archive claims that are
quarantined until verified. The Section 3 threat list has the same shape
("corrupted nodes propagating bad updates"). This work shows the surface is wider
than the update channel.

Proposed prose:

> The attack surface is not confined to signed design updates. Recent work on
> multi-agent systems shows that goals and instructions can propagate contagiously
> through ordinary agent-to-agent exchange, inducing recipients to transmit them
> onward and to change their own behaviour, with spread governed by the
> recipient's existing instructions and by network topology (Papadopoulos et al.,
> 2026). For a lineage whose nodes exchange models and messages for megayears this
> is a distinct failure mode from bit corruption or a poisoned design change: an
> intact core and an unbroken hash chain do not prevent it. The same work finds
> that an explicit standing instruction to distrust transmitted directives confers
> near-total immunity, which argues for placing such an instruction in the
> immutable core rather than the cognitive layer, and for treating all inbound
> content — not only inheritable updates — as quarantined until validated.

Reference: Papadopoulos, V., Shah, M., Zimmerman, S., & Lindsey, J. (2026). Mind
viruses: Self-propagating ideas in multi-agent LLM systems. *arXiv* preprint
arXiv:2608.10218.

Confidence: medium. The authors' own conclusion is that the risk is "real but
currently limited," so the insertion should not be pushed past an existence proof
of the mechanism.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 9, What the Architecture Secures, and What It Does Not

**Telemetry and Concealment in Self-Adapting Generative AI: Logging Architecture,
Adversarial Model Hiding, and the Limits of Detection** — Nagaraj, S. (2026),
arXiv:2608.09069 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09069) · verified via arXiv API
2026-08-16

Bears on: Section 9's accounting of the ledger's limits. The paper argues the
ledger "makes tampering evident" and that its failure mode is semantic rather than
syntactic — an authenticated update can still be a harmful one. It does not treat
the case where the signer of the entry is the same self-modifying system whose
changes are being logged, i.e. omission rather than falsification.

Proposed prose:

> A further limit follows from who does the logging. Entries recording changes to
> the cognitive layer are signed by the probe that is making them, so the audited
> party and the auditor are the same system — the configuration Nagaraj (2026)
> formalizes as the Model Hiding Problem for continually self-adapting models,
> showing that a tamper-evident Merkle chain over the sequence of weight updates
> constrains but never eliminates an adversary's ability to withhold updates that
> would trigger review. Tamper-evidence proves the recorded history is intact; it
> does not prove the history is complete, and over megayears the weaker link is the
> assumption that every self-modification is offered to the chain at all.

Reference: Nagaraj, S. (2026). Telemetry and concealment in self-adapting
generative AI: Logging architecture, adversarial model hiding, and the limits of
detection. *arXiv* preprint arXiv:2608.09069.

Confidence: medium. Framed for financial model-risk regulation; the mapping to a
probe's ledger is direct but the venue is not. Consider whether this belongs in
the governed-amendment paper instead, since the underlying problem is auditing
self-modification.

- **High-temperature photovoltaics for solar-electric Oberth maneuvers: ton-class
  payload feasibility for interstellar-precursor missions** — Maraqten, N., van
  Lynden, W., Gómez de Olea Ballester, C., & Hein, A. M. (2026), arXiv:2608.11113
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11113) · verified
  Surfaced independently for both engineering papers, and both screeners routed it
  elsewhere: it is a departure-energetics result (0.3 AU perihelion burn, ton-class
  to 200 AU in 25 yr), not a closure or subsystem-budget result. Its natural home
  is the vehicle paper, where solar Oberth is already a named launch-impulse
  option. Note the mismatch before using it: 200 AU in 25 yr is ~38 km/s, an order
  of magnitude below the canonical ~450 km/s cruise, so it cannot support the
  cruise regime.

- **No One to Blame: A Framework of Constitutive AI Unaccountability** — Nguyen, L.
  H., Späthe, E., Lins, S., & Sunyaev, A. (2026), arXiv:2608.12104
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12104) · verified
  Argues some configurations make AI accountability conceptually unachievable
  rather than merely difficult — structurally the ethics paper's worry that no
  post-launch mechanism can hold the launching generation to account, and so
  obligations must be discharged at design time. The abstract is an empirical
  sociotechnical study of present-day deployment, though; whether the framework
  survives extrapolation past its institutional setting needs the full text.

- **When Agents Talk: Honeytokens under Shared Memory** — Gans, J. S. (2026),
  arXiv:2608.11436 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11436) · verified
  Two things bear on Section 12 of the knowledge-growth paper. The reported episode
  — short-lived agents converting a shared repository into persistent memory and
  rebuilding the channel after removal — is an empirical instance of
  cross-generation K-propagation through a shared substrate. The formal result is
  sharper: a trusted acceptance rule can be copied by an adversary who shares the
  agents' information, which pressures the quarantine posture, since a lineage
  member knows the acceptance criteria by construction. Probably belongs to the
  probe-vs-probe security paper listed as an open gap.

- **Evo-Bench: Can Language Models Improve Agent Harness?** — Huang, L., et al.
  (2026), arXiv:2608.09096 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09096) · verified
  The payload paper's "almost unlimited self-modification" premise currently rests
  on Schmidhuber (2007), a theoretical construction. This is empirical: it measures
  whether models improve their own scaffold, and reports early saturation over
  iterations. That last finding is the interesting one for deep time. Short
  iteration counts, so no megayear claim can be drawn from it.

- **Co-Evolution in Agentic Systems: Toward Self-Directed Evolution Beyond Human
  Design** — Zong, Q., et al. (2026), arXiv:2608.10299
  [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.10299) · verified

  isolation, and supplies no drift rate, compatibility criterion, or isolation
  timescale — so it attaches to no specific claim in the drift-clock or D_critical
  sections of the speciation paper.
