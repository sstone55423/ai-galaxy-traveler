### Governed-amendment paper — Section 11, The Conservative Default, Defended as a Strategy

**Constitutional drafting processes and constitutional success** — Chilton, A.,
Mungan, M. C., & Versteeg, M. (2026), *Public Choice*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11127-026-01447-w) · verified via OpenAlex and
Crossref (journal-article, Public Choice, issued 2026-08-22; authors confirmed)

Bears on: the section's strategy of shrinking the domain over which amendment is
ever needed, and on Section 5's taxonomy of founding error.

Chilton et al. model constitution-drafting as a game among representatives of
interest groups. The probability of constitutional success is single-peaked in
the degree of issue protection while each group's payoff rises with it, so the
equilibrium entrenches more than the success-maximizing amount; per-representative
protection falls as the number of groups rises, but total protection rises. A
more inclusive founding therefore yields a larger core and a lower probability of
success. That is a founding error distinct from the three the amendment paper
names — not a wrong value but an oversized core, produced by the drafting
procedure rather than by any drafter's mistake — and under immutability it locks
in with everything else. It also sharpens the exchange with the ethics paper: the
broad participation its consent argument requires is the same mechanism that
inflates the residue Section 11 exists to shrink.

Held at **medium confidence** and not staged: the transfer is analogical (human
constitutions), so this is an author judgement rather than a mechanical
insertion. The screening agent's proposed prose is preserved above in
paraphrase; the argument survives Section 8's standing objection that
constitutional analogies import a missing enforcement context, because the result
concerns drafting incentives rather than enforcement.
