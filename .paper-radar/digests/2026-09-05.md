### Bootstrapping paper — Section 3, The Bootstrapping Cascade

**Characterization of flow behavior of graded regolith-metal powder mixtures** —
Mahid, S. M., Jones, I., & Kouraytem, N. (2026), *Granular Matter* 28
[doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10035-026-01665-5) · verified via Crossref and
OpenAlex (authors, title, venue, 2026-09-02 all match)

Bears on: the honest-bounds caveat that a printer collapses part-specific
tooling into information but that "its feedstock preparation is a process chain
of its own." Section 3 currently leans on two 2026 regolith-AM results
(Malekpour and Hojjati on PEKK-regolith composites, McCallum et al. on
binder-free laser sintering), both of which make local feedstock look drop-in.
This one cuts the other way.

> Feedstock preparation is where that recursion reappears. Mahid et al. (2026)
> report lunar highland simulant poorly suited to powder-bed processes because
> of its low flowability, and recover flow by blending in spherical stainless-
> steel powder — 50 vol% giving a substantial drop in dynamic angle of repose,
> coarser particle distributions helping most, and packing quality moving
> independently of flow behaviour. Powder-bed AM on local material therefore
> presupposes a prior capability, the production of spherical metal powder,
> placing the printer a rung higher on the ladder than raw regolith abundance
> alone suggests.

Reference: Mahid, S. M., Jones, I., & Kouraytem, N. (2026). Characterization of
flow behavior of graded regolith–metal powder mixtures. *Granular Matter*, 28.
https://doi.org/10.1007/s10035-026-01665-5

Confidence: medium. The abstract shows 50 vol% works, not that it is the
minimum; the prose above claims only what the abstract supports.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — Section 9, What the Architecture Secures, and What It Does Not

**From Sealed to Anchored: What a Consortium Ledger Adds to HMAC-Sealed IoT
Audit Logs in Ambient Assisted Living** — Gawande, K., & Stantchev, V. (2026),
*Applied Sciences* 16(17), 8782
[doi](https://doi.org/10.3390/app16178782) · verified via Crossref and OpenAlex
(authors, title, venue, 2026-09-03 all match)

Bears on: the scope claims that self-verification is "not proof against a node
that has been fully compromised" (Section 7) and that, for cognitive-layer
entries, "the audited party and the auditor are the same system" (Section 9).
The paper states this defect informally and never names the property that is
actually missing.

> The defect generalizes beyond the logging case. Work separating integrity from
> evidential strength decomposes the latter into six properties — integrity,
> authenticity, non-repudiation of the anchored history, third-party
> verifiability, temporal upper-bounding, and bounded completeness — and
> observes that a log a party seals under its own key establishes nothing to an
> outside auditor beyond that party's own honesty; the remedy is to anchor
> batched Merkle roots with a party that does not hold the key, at a cost
> amortized to under a byte of anchored state per record at batch sizes of
> ~10^3 (Gawande & Stantchev, 2026). A settled probe has no such party available
> in real time: its only external anchors are sibling settlements, reachable at
> years to millennia of latency, which is what Section 6's requirement of
> independent cross-lineage confirmation for high-impact changes actually buys —
> not faster finality, but the third-party verifiability a self-sealed ledger
> cannot supply on its own.

Reference: Gawande, K., & Stantchev, V. (2026). From sealed to anchored: What a
consortium ledger adds to HMAC-sealed IoT audit logs in ambient assisted living.
*Applied Sciences*, 16(17), 8782. https://doi.org/10.3390/app16178782

Confidence: medium. Their mechanism is symmetric (HMAC) where the paper's is
signature-based; the third-party-verifiability gap is key-model-independent, and
the prose is worded to cover both. This is a rare ledger-security hit that is
not blockchain boosterism — it supplies a formal name for a gap the paper
already concedes twice, and explains Section 6's cross-lineage quorum in
security terms rather than merely consensus terms.
