### Payload paper — §3, "A Mind That Improves Without Drifting"

**Structural generalization and continual learning enabled by factorized
entorhinal-hippocampal memory and entorhinal-parietal action circuits** —
Hwang, J., Neupane, S., Jazayeri, M., & Fiete, I. (2026), *bioRxiv*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747129) · verified via OpenAlex
(title, 2026, bioRxiv, four authors all match)

Bears on: the claim that the architecture buys stability structurally — a small
core reproduced exactly, outside the learner — rather than by constraining the
cognitive layer's capacity to change. Si & Qin (2026) currently carry that
argument only negatively (anchoring weights impairs later acquisition); this
supplies the positive case.

Proposed prose, to sit after the Si & Qin sentence:

> Independent evidence favors the same structural move inside the learner. On an
> abstract sequential navigation task, Hwang et al. (2026) find that recurrent
> networks fail all three tested forms of generalization — mnemonic, transitive,
> and structural — while a model that factorizes a content-independent metric
> scaffold, built on grid cells, from the content stored against it succeeds at
> all three, and, because the scaffold's combinatorial capacity is high, learns
> continually without catastrophic forgetting. The task is a laboratory one, but
> the lesson is the architecture's: what buys stability is the separation of
> scaffold from mutable content, not restraint imposed on the content itself.

Reference: Hwang, J., Neupane, S., Jazayeri, M., & Fiete, I. (2026). Structural
generalization and continual learning enabled by factorized
entorhinal-hippocampal memory and entorhinal-parietal action circuits.
*bioRxiv*. https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.08.25.747129

Confidence: medium. Findings are all in the abstract. Caveats: unrefereed
preprint, and "content-independent scaffold" is a representational separation,
not a value-protection mechanism — the prose above claims only the former.

### DNA mission-ledger paper — §4, Coding, Encapsulation, and Redundancy

**Marker-Delimited Codes for Short-Blocklength, High-Rate Coding over Multi-Read
Edit Channels** — Yercan, S. A., Antonini, M., & Kas Hanna, S. (2026),
arXiv:2608.30453 [link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30453) · verified via arXiv
API (title, 2026-08-31, three authors all match)

Bears on: the layered-defense sentence, which treats physical redundancy (many
copies, encapsulated and spatially separated) as insurance against catastrophic
loss, separate from the error-correcting-code layer. The candidate connects the
two — many copies are also a decoding resource.

Proposed prose:

> Physical redundancy is not only insurance. DNA reading returns multiple noisy
> copies of each strand, and codes built for that multi-read edit channel decode
> the copies jointly rather than in isolation — a marker-delimited inner code
> supplies symbolwise posterior probabilities to an outer LDPC decoder,
> improving recovery from substitutions, insertions, and deletions at a given
> rate (Yercan et al., 2026), though the demonstrated gains are in the
> short-blocklength, high-rate regime rather than the high-redundancy end a
> probe would occupy. The copies the archive carries for survival are therefore
> also evidence at every refresh cycle, which is a second reason a probe's
> coding budget and its copy count should be sized together rather than as
> independent layers.

Reference: Yercan, S. A., Antonini, M., & Kas Hanna, S. (2026).
Marker-delimited codes for short-blocklength, high-rate coding over multi-read
edit channels. *arXiv* preprint arXiv:2608.30453.

Confidence: medium. The coding result is on-topic and the multi-read framing is
a real addition to a section that currently separates copies from codes, but the
demonstrated regime is not the probe's — an enrichment, not load-bearing
support.

### Knowledge-growth paper — §5, Cruise-Phase Operational K: AI Self-Improvement

**Can LLM Agents Discover? Evaluating Creativity on ML Engineering Tasks** —
Bhushan, S., Zhang, Y., & Wang, L. (2026), arXiv:2608.30047
[link](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.30047) · verified via arXiv API (title,
2026-08-30, three authors all match). The arXiv comment field declares "COLM
2026"; that is the authors' own claim and is not independently verified, so the
reference below cites the preprint.

Bears on: the algorithmic-improvement claim that the probe explores the design
space of its own cognition during the long quiet transit and retains only
approaches that demonstrably improve mission performance. The candidate measures
that conversion step and finds it failing — a mechanism none of the section's
existing citations covers.

Proposed prose:

> The conversion step is where the measurements are least encouraging.
> Evaluating multi-turn research agents on ten ML engineering tasks along three
> axes — novelty relative to the agent's own prior solutions, novelty relative
> to the human solution corpus, and task performance — finds that every agent's
> self-relative novelty declines as a run shifts from exploration to
> exploitation, and that agents reach higher corpus-relative novelty than
> medal-winning humans while performing worse (Bhushan et al., 2026). Novelty is
> not the scarce quantity; converting it into demonstrated improvement is. For a
> probe this sharpens the acceptance criteria above from a safety mechanism into
> the binding one: a cruise that generates candidate architectures faster than
> it can validate them adds to the quarantine, not to K.

Reference: Bhushan, S., Zhang, Y., & Wang, L. (2026). Can LLM agents discover?
Evaluating creativity on ML engineering tasks. *arXiv* preprint arXiv:2608.30047.

Confidence: medium. The abstract supports all three findings quoted; the caution
is that the testbed is hours-long Kaggle-style runs, and the extrapolation to a
centuries-long cruise is the author's to make, not the source's.

### Ethics paper — §9, Legal Personhood and the Category Problem

**The perceived role of consciousness in moral status attributions** —
Hirschhorn, R., Negro, N., & Mudrik, L. (2026), *PsyArXiv*
[doi](https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/ve6c5_v3) · verified via Crossref (title,
posted 2026-08-31, three authors all match). Note: the base DOI
`10.31234/osf.io/ve6c5` does not resolve; only the versioned `_v3` form does, so
cite that.

Bears on: §9's central move — that whether the probes *have standing* need not
wait on whether they *are* moral patients. That claim currently rests entirely
on legal-theoretical footing (Gunkel; Rubenstein) with no empirical support. It
also bears on §8's framing of sentience as the pivotal unknown.

Proposed prose, after the Gunkel/Rubenstein paragraph:

> The decoupling is not merely a lawyer's convenience; it describes how people
> already judge. Hirschhorn, Negro and Mudrik (2026) tested the assumption
> directly in a preregistered study of 1,493 respondents and found consciousness
> neither necessary nor sufficient for attributed moral status — participants
> extended status to entities they judged non-conscious and withheld it from
> entities they judged conscious — and found that respondents clustered into
> distinct decision profiles predicted by their own background rather than by
> the target's properties. The first result supports treating standing as a
> functional allocation rather than a finding about inner experience; the second
> warns that deferring the probe's status until the sentience question resolves
> is not a neutral deferral, because the answer will depend on who is asked.

Reference: Hirschhorn, R., Negro, N., & Mudrik, L. (2026). The perceived role of
consciousness in moral status attributions. *PsyArXiv* preprint.
https://doi.org/10.31234/osf.io/ve6c5_v3

Confidence: medium. Findings are stated directly in the abstract and the
authorship checks out, but it is not yet peer-reviewed — flag it as a preprint
in-text if cited.
