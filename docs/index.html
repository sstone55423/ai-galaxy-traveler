What has to be solved first

The concept is physically coherent. Buildable is a different question. These are the unsolved problems standing between the papers and a launchable artifact.

In-flight electronics repair — the probe must fabricate replacement chips from asteroid feedstock, autonomously, with no human support. Nothing like this exists.
AI stability across millions of years — no AI has operated without human correction for more than a few years. A formal theory of value-preserving self-modification does not yet exist.
Governed amendment — if the immutable core encodes a founding error, there is no correction mechanism across a million-year lineage.
Reactor longevity — the best small space fission reactor ever tested ran for 28 hours. The probe needs thousands of years.
Power conversion without moving parts — Stirling generators wear out. Thermophotovoltaics are the leading alternative but unproven at the required duration.
Transit-proof archive — cosmic rays shred DNA over centuries. Five-dimensional quartz optical storage is the best near-term substitute.
Braking at the target — the magnetic sail is theoretically sound and has never been tested at any scale. An electric sail is a credible backup; the braking physics needs formal analysis.
Getting to speed — nothing heavier than a few grams has been accelerated to interstellar velocity. A lunar mass driver is the most buildable path to 150–200 km/s.
