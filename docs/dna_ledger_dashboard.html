What this proves, and what it does not. Any alteration to a committed block invalidates every later hash — tampering is evident, and recovered strands carrying the chain are self-verifying. But this is syntactic integrity and provenance only: the chain proves the recorded history intact, not complete — an update withheld from the chain leaves no trace — and an unbroken chain does not prove meaning preserved or behavior faithful.

Two regimes: passive vault vs settlement refresh

Anchors, loosely: matched-temperature ancient-DNA decay gives a ~521-year half-life for a 242 bp fragment in bone at ~13 °C; encapsulated-and-cold extrapolations reach far longer, at −18 °C into the millennia-to-megayear range. The paper treats these as loose bounds, not design values — hence the two regimes: cruise is passive by necessity (synthesis and sequencing hardware is powered down), and only a settlement can run the active loop that makes the archive effectively immortal. Redundancy + error-correcting codes tolerate heavy strand loss before content loss; this chart shows raw strand survival, the pessimistic floor.
