Dust Damage vs Cruise Speed

Impact energy from interstellar dust grains scales as v², and power deposited scales as v³ (flux increases with v too). Slow probes are not a concession — they are a structural advantage. This dashboard quantifies the penalty paid by faster designs.

Shield mass penalty at 20% c

At 20% c (60,000 km/s) a grain strikes with 17,778× more energy than at 450 km/s. Shield mass scales faster still — faster probes need proportionally more shielding, not less. The mass budget consumed by shielding crowds out the manufacturing plant, undercutting self-replication closure.

Why slow wins

At 450 km/s a small interstellar grain (10⁻¹⁴ kg) delivers about 1 J — manageable with modest shielding depth. The v³ power scaling means doubling cruise speed eightfolds the shield heat load. The 450 km/s design sits at a practical optimum: fast enough to reach Proxima in ~2,800 yr, slow enough that dust is a solved engineering problem rather than a mission-ending one.

Key reference points

  • Parker Solar Probe — 192 km/s: fastest human-made object; KE ratio 0.182× baseline; power ratio 0.078× baseline.
  • Our probe — 450 km/s (baseline): KE ratio 1.00×; power ratio 1.00×; ~1 J per small grain; ~2,800 yr to Proxima Centauri.
  • 1% c — 3,000 km/s: KE ratio 44.4×; power ratio 296×; 210 yr to Proxima.
  • 10% c — 30,000 km/s: KE ratio 4,444×; power ratio 296,000×; 42 yr to Proxima. Shield mass dominates vehicle mass budget.
  • 20% c — 60,000 km/s: KE ratio 17,778×; power ratio 2.37 × 10⁶×; 21 yr to Proxima. Erosion rate makes multi-millimeter shields mandatory, precluding self-replication closure.
